## Замечания критика

The base is strong. I would fix/qualify the following before translation starts.

## Specific decisions

- **Хор: use “the Choir,” not “the Chorus.”**  
  Correct for live voices, miners’ choir, and **the Choirmaster**. “Chorus” suggests Greek theater or a song refrain. Caveat: “Choir” can sound angelic/churchy, so ground it early in the miners’ choir.

- **Дрейф: keep “the Drift.”**  
  It works. Avoid using *drift* for unrelated future tech: not “lane drift,” but **lane departure** / “the car chirped when I crossed the line.”

- **Откат: keep “rewind,” but fix collocations.**  
  Good because of the cassette metaphor. But **“the rewind won’t reach”** is clumsy. Use:  
  - “The tape won’t rewind that far.”  
  - “The rewind doesn’t go back far enough.”  
  - UI: **rewind depth** is fine.

- **Водяной: keep “Vodyanoy” as nickname.**  
  Do not translate to *Waterboy* / *Water Man* as the nickname: *Waterboy* is comic/American sports; *waterman* means boatman. First mention should gloss it hard:  
  > the vodyanoy — the Water Man, the old thing under the river that grabs children by the ankles.  
  Creature: **the vodyanoy** lowercase. Nickname: **Vodyanoy**, no article.  
  Later plural **The Vodyanoys** is awkward but preserves the rhyme; **Water Rats** only if the chapter is intentionally using a new insult.

- **“Synchronize watches.”: keep exactly.**  
  It is clipped, military, engraved-English. Do not add *your*. Do not use *sync*.  
  Better explanation of 12.04/04:12:  
  > The twelfth of April, ’61. Gagarin’s day. Written our way: 12.04. Turn the day and month around and you get 04:12 — four-twelve.

- **HEART vs EMPATHY: I’d keep HEART, with a tooltip.**  
  **BODY / MIND / HEART / WILL** feels native and warm; **EMPATHY** is precise but clinical/UI-ish. But make clear in the Guide: **Heart means empathy, not health or romance.** Avoid heart icons if possible.

- **American vs British English:**  
  Use **American spelling and punctuation**, but not American cultural voice. Keep metric, rubles, 24-hour time, Russian date order in headers. Where a hard vocabulary choice is needed, choose neutral AmE: **Mom, Dad, Granddad, sidewalk, truck, apartment, vice principal, passing**. Avoid British leakage: *Mum, Grandad, lorry, lift, overtaking*.

## Glossary fixes / watch points

- **Grandad → Granddad** for AmE consistency. Or use **Grandpa** only in very childlike direct address.
- **“Tisha?..” → “Tisha…?”** English punctuation should not use Russian `?..`.
- **the Choir Notebook** sounds like a choir rehearsal book. In prose use **the Choir’s Notebook** or **Granddad’s notebook**; UI **The Notebook** is fine.
- **Tuning Fork:** “struck the tuning fork on himself” is not idiomatic. Use **tested Tuning Fork on himself**, **made himself the tuning fork**, or **rang the tuning fork in his own head**, depending on context.
- **the Wager** is good if you want a Pascal’s Wager echo. If not, **the Bet** is plainer and harsher. “Stake” is not only “bonfire,” but **the Stake** sounds odd; **the stakes** is idiomatic.
- **Experiment 42 / Forty-Two** will strongly evoke *The Hitchhiker’s Guide to the Galaxy* in English. If unwanted, prefer **Experiment 42** over bare chapter title **Forty-Two**.
- **Shturmanskie:** real-world English often uses **Sturmanskie**. If you keep transliteration, say **a Shturmanskie watch**, not “a Shturmanskie.”
- **storm jacket** is understandable but stiff. Better first mention: **a canvas windbreaker** / **a canvas storm jacket**. Avoid “I reckon” unless you want rural/dialect color; use “Must be Granddad’s.”
- **old piles → old pilings.** “Pilings” is the natural river/bridge word.
- **willow scrub → willow thickets** is more idiomatic.
- **Black Backwater** is usable, but heavy. Do not switch to **Blackwater**: that has PMC / fantasy associations.
- **the default**: never bare on first use. Use **the 1998 default** / **the ’98 default, when the ruble fell through the floor**. A child should parrot it as an adult word.
- **aid flour** is not idiomatic. Use **flour from the aid boxes** or **relief flour**.
- **coke plant → coking plant** if industrial precision matters.
- **semi** is AmE and okay, but **tractor-trailer** is more neutral.
- **Fix Price — the dollar store** is very American. Better: **Fix Price, the discount chain** / **one-price discount store**.
- **Sektor Gaza** needs “the punk band” nearby now; otherwise English readers may think of Gaza politics.
- For bands/brands, use conventional English spellings where established: **Splean**, **Mumiy Troll**, probably **Ruki Vverh!** rather than strict *Vverkh*.
- **What I took away** sounds corporate-debrief. For the lesson card, use **What I Learned** or, more literary, **What I Brought Back**.
- Scene titles:
  - **The Dry Bed → The Dry Riverbed**.
  - **Overtaking → Passing** / **The Pass**.
  - **Tired → Fatigue** or **Exhaustion**, unless deliberately child-simple.
  - **Зорька:** check context. If it is a name, keep **Zorka**. If dawn, **First Light** is fine.
  - **The Buoy Keeper** is acceptable; **The Buoyman** is more folkloric.

## Register notes

The age rules are mostly right, but avoid fake American kid-slang.

### 7–10
Do not use **wha’?**, **like, ever**, **as if**, **duh** except maybe once as a joke. They Americanize the child. Use plain schoolyard English: **Huh? So? Bet you can’t. No way. Can too.**

A child may say “I’m scared.” The rule should be: he does not analyze fear like an adult.

Good line:
> I’m seven. The Tom is black by the old pilings. Vitka says, “Bet you can’t make it.” I say, “Can too,” and count my toes before I step in.

### 11–14
The suggested slang works if used lightly: **lame, no way, loser, chicken, shut up, sucks**. Do not make them sound like American TV kids.

Good line:
> Shmelyov says our Dendy is trash, then begs for one more round. “Move over.” “No way. You died.”

### 15–18
**busted, brutal, hardcore** are period-plausible. Good ban list: *epic fail, cringe, vibe, based, low-key, trauma, red flag*.  
“Literally” existed, but now feels modern/AI-ish as an intensifier, so banning it is still wise.

Good line:
> ICQ blinks green. Nika writes, You coming? I type yeah, erase it, type later. If Dad sees the phone, I’m busted.

### Adult / old man
Good direction. Keep it dry and concrete. Do not over-ban past perfect; one anchoring **had** is sometimes necessary.

Good adult line:
> After the shift, I wash twice. Coal stays in the cracks anyway. The car chirps when I cross the line; I tell it I’m awake.

Good old-man aside:
> Forty years old, that “doctor.” Looked sixty. In ’96 everybody did.

## Add to the English AI-tic stop-list

Add these; they will creep in:

- Filter verbs: **I saw / I heard / I felt / I noticed / I realized** when direct description is stronger.
- Stock body reactions: **my heart pounded, breath caught, stomach dropped, lump in my throat, blood ran cold, goosebumps rose**.
- Stock death prose: **everything went black, darkness swallowed me, the last thing I saw was…**
- Cinematic filler: **for a beat, in slow motion, the world narrowed, the camera/framed/panned**.
- Atmosphere clichés: **the air was thick/heavy, tension hung in the air, shadows danced, silence pressed in**.
- Hedge-filler: **somehow, almost, just, seemed to, kind of, I don’t know why**.
- Dialogue tics: overuse of **murmured, whispered, breathed, chuckled, smirked**.
- Aphoristic hindsight: **That was the day I learned…**, **There are moments when…**, **Memory is a funny thing…**
- Modern meme syntax: **yeah, no; I mean; because X; that happened; nope** unless deliberately period-appropriate in dialogue.
