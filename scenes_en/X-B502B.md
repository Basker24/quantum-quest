Tolik is standing chest-deep in the water, guffawing. I wade out to him, grab his arm, pull him toward the shore. And then he stops guffawing.

"Ow," he says in surprise. And then he roars, without words, like an animal.

Something has stung him under the water. He climbs me like a pole, arms around my neck, legs around my waist, and we both go under. He weighs about a hundred kilos. I try to twist free, and he holds on harder, pushing down on my shoulders and shoving himself up with me. He doesn't mean it; he just wants to breathe.

Under the water it's dark, and above, the moon glows as a cloudy blur. Shadows move past the blur. Those are Tolik's legs, thrashing the water above my head. Something stings my leg too, and I open my mouth.

The water comes into me salty and warm, like soup. I hear the beat from the shore, muffled, through the depth of water, and under the beat something is whispering, many voices at once, close, like under the snag long ago. The last thing is the smell of Mom. Coal dust, though there's no coal here.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing on the sand again, the bucket at my feet, and Tolik is yelling to me from the water about fresh milk.

My neck aches, my shoulders ache, as if a sack of cement had just been sitting on them. I touch my neck and feel fingers on it, his fingers, though Tolik is twenty meters away.

He drowned me and didn't notice. They pulled him out, and not me. Drunk, in the border guards' arms, he'll be calling "Andryukha!" and looking at the shore.
