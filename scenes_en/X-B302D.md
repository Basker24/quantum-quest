The boulder is wet, but I climb up, clinging to the cracks, and stand on its flat top. From here I can see the whole saddle: the cedar, the boulder field, the hollow where brown water is already running and knocking rocks along. It won't reach me. I'm higher than everything. I think I'm laughing out loud.

The hammer above my shoulder is already shrieking. The hair on my head stands up; I feel it rising under my hood, and my nose smells of a socket.

White comes straight into me, from above, into the crown of my head. I don't have time to notice pain, only loudness, so loud that sound becomes light and light becomes sound. I'm not on the boulder, and then I am, lying on my side on wet rock, and smoke is rising from my boots. The hand I was holding the strap with is black. My heart has stopped. It smells of scorched canvas and coal dust from Mom's hair.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing on the saddle in the downpour again, and the hammer above my shoulder is humming like a transformer.

I remember how the hammer shrieked, and how it smelled of a socket. I remember the whole saddle below me and nothing higher than me.

The water in the hollow was carrying rocks; that much I remembered right. But what I made of it was stupid. Climbing up high in a thunderstorm means turning yourself into the cedar. Below the water is no good, above everything is no good either. You need a place in the middle.
