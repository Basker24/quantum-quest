I run over the ties with the self-rescuer in my mouth. Up top I can run eight hundred meters in three minutes; I ran it in PE at college. Down here every tie is higher than the last. The mouthpiece rubs my gums, my mouth is all rubber and sourness, and breathing through it is like breathing through a pillow.

At four hundred meters I start counting ties so I won't count breaths. At five hundred I find I'm on my knees and don't remember getting down on them. I get up. I take another twenty steps and lie down, carefully, on my side, the way you lie down on a couch.

My lamp shines on the roof, on a rusty lagging bar with "520" written on it in chalk. I really want to sleep, and I give myself five minutes. For some reason Mom is sitting next to me, saying her hair smells of coal again, however much she washes it.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing at the face again. The gas detector is beeping, and Seryoga is swearing forty meters behind me.

My legs are buzzing, the way they did after that run. I look at them, at my boots, at the wet floor under them. Not a single sleeper yet.

I remember the "520" on the lagging as if I'd read it out loud. Eight hundred meters, it turns out, is a lot for me. Up top I didn't know that.
