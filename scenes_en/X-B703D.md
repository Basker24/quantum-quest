I know how much time I have, and I run. On the mirror ice the stroller rolls by itself, faster than me, pulling the handle out of my hands. Under the wall, in the middle of the footpath, my felt boot slides out from under me, and I sit down hard on the ice, right on my tailbone, still holding the handle.

I'm sitting exactly where I lay last time. There's a short crack overhead. The icicle falls when it's supposed to, not a second earlier. It doesn't know I was running.

The blow lands on my shoulder and head. I let go of the handle, and the stroller rolls on, downhill. I lie on my side and watch it bounce over the ruts until I can't see anymore.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing at the corner of the building again, holding the stroller handle, and Varya smacks her lips in her sleep.

I remember my boot sliding out. My tailbone aches, though I haven't sat on it today. The icicle fell to the minute, just as I remembered, only I ended up under it again, because nobody runs on ice like that.

I remembered the time right, and I was wrong to think I'd make it. You don't go under that wall at all, not walking, not running. I look up, and the icicle is hanging there, long, as tall as I am, and the sun breaks inside it.
