The crosscut is dark and quiet and smells of old rock, like a root cellar. I sit down with my back against the cold stopping and wait. The self-rescuer hisses, my mouth full of rubber. By the book you're supposed to wait calmly, not move, save oxygen, and that's what I do. I sit with my hands on my knees, like at a school assembly.

The air in the crosscut stands still, the way it does in a three-liter jar. Gas seeps in from the drift above, quiet as a cat slipping into the kitchen. I don't notice until my arms go limp and the self-rescuer feels heavy and useless, like a winter hat in May.

I take it off. Right away I feel good, warm and calm, like after a banya. Behind my back is the stopping, and in it a door, and behind the door a fresh wind is blowing. I don't know about the door. It's the last thing I don't know.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing at the face again, and the gas detector is beeping.

My shoulder blades remember the rough concrete. Cold. I spent that whole hour with my back pressed to it and never once turned around.

What was there, behind my back? I don't know. I never looked. I never really studied the ventilation plan hanging in the shift office; there was never time.
