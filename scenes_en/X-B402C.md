I set the box down on the snow and step into the light with my hands up.

"Mister! I'm just a loader! They ditched me, I've got nothing to do with this!"

The flashlight hits me in the eyes, and I see nothing but white. Somewhere on the man's belt a radio crackles: "Kuzmich, what's going on? Kuzmich!" I take a step toward him, to show I'm empty-handed. Hands up, gloves on.

He doesn't see the gloves. He sees a man coming at him out of the dark.

A crash, and I'm sat down on the ice, and my chest goes hot and wet. The man screams in a thin voice, like a woman: "Why'd you come out?! Why'd you come out, you idiot?!" He's on his knees over me, pressing his glove to my chest. His voice is hoarse, like Dad's after a shift. It smells of Mom's hair, and then of nothing.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing in the doorway of the cold room again with the forty-second box in my arms, and the flashlight is crawling across the yard.

I remember the voice. "Kuzmich, what's going on?" I remember how he knelt over me screaming "why'd you come out." He didn't mean to. He was more scared than I was; he shot at the darkness that came at him.

Kuzmich. Dad's crew leader, Gennady Kuzmin; they pensioned him off from the Glubokaya for his lungs. He drank tea at our place on Dad's birthday. He argued with Dad about Metallurg until he was hoarse, and that hoarseness is what I hear now from the dark.
