It's dry under the cedar and smells of resin. I sit down on the needles with my back to the trunk, hug my pack and watch the rain pour off the branches in front of me like a wall, and beyond the wall there's nothing to see, only white. It even makes me laugh how cozy I am here, like in the hut behind the garages.

I don't hear the strike. I see all the needles under my palms flare blue, every single needle, and then someone enormous grabs me by the back, by the spine, and squeezes, the way you squeeze a sparrow in your fist. It comes from below, from the roots, through the ground, through me. My legs jerk on their own; the pack flies off.

I'm lying on my side and can't breathe in. My heart beats once in the wrong place, high up, by my throat, and doesn't beat again. In front of my eyes are needles, wet, with steam rising off them. It smells scorched. It smells of coal dust, like Mom's hair when she comes home from a shift and leans over me.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing on the saddle in the downpour again, and the hammer above my shoulder is humming like a transformer.

My back is still cramping, all along my spine, as if someone had drawn a red-hot wire down it. I remember that it struck the cedar, but it killed me through the roots. Its roots run across the rock in every direction, under the lichen, a long way. I was sitting by the trunk, right in the middle.

The soles of my feet are tingling, like a leg that's gone to sleep. The lichen under them is dry, and under it are roots, thick as an arm, stretching from the cedar across the whole clearing.
