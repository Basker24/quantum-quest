In the gap the ceiling settles on my back. I crawl and breathe. The air is sweet, like Buratino soda, like the soda from the vending machine on Sovetsky when I was a kid.

In the middle of the gap the silt suddenly turns colorful. Orange, red, green, as if someone had switched on a string of Christmas lights. My temples throb so hard I can't hear my own breathing. I feel like laughing. I don't remember why I'm here or where I'm crawling to. The rope is in my hand, and I let go of it, because I don't need to hold it anymore, and why I don't need to, I don't know.

I'm warm and sleepy. Mom used to say you mustn't sleep on rocks, you'll catch cold. I lay my cheek on the rock and close my eyes, just for a minute.

The regulator slips out of my mouth by itself, water comes into my mouth, and I don't care anymore. Someone is whispering, many voices at once. The last thing is the smell of Mom, coal dust, and something sweet, like soda.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing at the mouth of the squeeze again, knee-deep in rust-colored slurry, and the marine is pointing at me.

My temples throb. I squeeze my head in my hands and stand like that until the marine asks if I'm all right.

I remember how sweet the air was in there and how colorful the silt, and how I let go of the rope because it all seemed funny. The air in my tanks is from Chalok, from Somchai's, and Somchai's generator stands by the window.

In my mouth is the taste of exhaust, gasoline, sweetish. Spring two years ago, Koh Tao, Somchai's shed, I'm counting half price on my fingers, and the generator is rattling by the window, and I'm looking right at it.
