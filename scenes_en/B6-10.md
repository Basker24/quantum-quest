For the third summer running there's been no rain in the mountains. The moss crunches underfoot like rusks; the spring by the cabin has dropped by half. Stepan, when he came in June, said everything is burning down below: near Tashtagol, near Myski, along the highway. There's no one to put it out. Half the forest rangers were taken last autumn.

"The Wager, damn it," said Stepan, and spat. "The ones on top decided they won't die. Bought themselves a second chance and put us on the table."

I hear about the Wager even without Stepan. Since last year the strangers in my pitch have sounded like a disturbed beehive. They rewind ten times a day, generals, ministers, and with every rewind they care less about what happens to everyone else. The world creeps from it like wet paper. On Stepan's radio people argue about whether there was a bridge in Novokuznetsk last year or whether it was never built.

Since lunch the smell of burning has been stronger than usual. At three o'clock smoke rises up over the valley from below, rust-colored, dense, the whole width of the valley. The wind pulls it uphill, toward me. You can hear it crackle. It's a ground fire coming, through the dry moss, and above it fire leaps through the treetops.

The Choir screams all at once, and you can't make out anything in it.

To the right, above the forest, is the bald peak. Bare rock, a boulder field; nothing to burn there. A kilometer uphill. To the left, down the valley, is the river, but between me and the river are yesterday's burn and the fire itself. By the cabin runs the creek.

## После B
In my head is a picture from a school safety textbook I haven't thought of in forty years: an arrow going up a slope, a little stick figure, and the arrow overtaking the figure. And fire has nothing to walk on over ground that's already burned.

I run downhill, toward the smoke, past the cabin, into the burn. The ground there is black and warm, like stove ash; stumps smolder here and there, but there's nothing left to burn. The fire passes over my head, through the crowns, through the living pines on the slope, roaring like a train over a crossing. I lie face down in the ash and breathe through my wet shirt.

I reach the river toward evening, black all over. I sit in the water up to my waist until morning. In the morning I climb back up. The cabin is standing. The fire passed above it, up the slope, toward the bald peak, and the peak is covered in soot to its very top.

## После C
I shove the sheepskin into the font and lie down in the water, on the stones, face up. I cover myself with the coat, head and all. The water is icy; it gets down my collar, into my trousers, into my ears.

The fire passes over me in about twenty minutes. I hear the cabin crackle, its glass burst, its roof fall in. Hot things pour down on the coat, and the coat hisses. Under it I breathe through a gap right at the water, and the air there is hot, but there's enough of it.

When I climb out, the cabin is gone. No bunk, no stove, no woodpile, no letters from Sonka from twenty-five years. I sit on a rock, wet, and shake until morning. In the morning I run a fever, and my legs won't hold me. Stepan finds me three days later by the creek and is silent for a long time.

## После D
I stand by the cabin looking at the smoke. I remember running uphill and how the moss caught fire under my feet before the fire even got there. I close my eyes and listen. Somewhere close, on a neighboring fork, there's a world where the wind is about to turn. Very close, half a step away.

The wind turns. The smoke lies over on its side, into the next valley, and the fire goes there, through the dry moss, past me. An hour later I see Stepan's winter hut burning over there beyond the spur, empty, it's summer.

## После E
I remember how the moss burned on the slope and how the sheepskin burned in the creek. So, not up, and not here. I run downhill, toward the smoke, past the cabin, into yesterday's black burn, not picking my way. Stumps smolder; underfoot is hot ash, and the soles of my boots start to smell of rubber.

The fire passes higher, through the crowns, through the living pines, roaring. I lie face down in the ash, and above me it hums so loudly I can't hear the Choir. Then it goes quiet, with only crackling up above.

I crawl out to the river at dusk, burned on the hands, and sit in the water until morning. My knees have ached before bad weather ever since.
