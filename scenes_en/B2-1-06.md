The R-255, the Siberia highway. From Novosibirsk to Yurga, two hundred kilometers, straight as a ruler. At three in the morning there's nobody on it but semis. After Bolotnoye fog rises from the hollows, and within twenty minutes I'm driving through milk. Visibility about thirty meters. Ahead, in the white, float the red lights of a semi, blurred, like a cigarette in somebody else's hand.

At four-twelve they'll find the empty bed. Then they'll pull the camera footage from the checkpoints. I have to make it to Yurga by five; there's the turn-off for Tomsk, and they won't look for me there.

The parcel box from Kemerovo is on the passenger seat. I hold it with my right hand on the curves, the way you hold a child.

I remember the cage at the Glubokaya, seventeen years ago, and the voice in the Choir shouting "Truck!" in the fog.

## После B
I slow to sixty and stay behind the red lights, about fifty meters back. They lead me through the fog like a lamp in a mine. The semi drives steadily; its driver knows this road better than I do.

At one forty-two the lights suddenly flare brighter and spread apart. The semi stops, it skids, but it stops. I brake twenty meters short of its rear doors. Ahead, in the white, a second semi is standing across the road, and nobody gets out of its cab.

The driver of the first one jumps out with a flashlight and runs over there, swearing so loud I can hear it through the glass. While he's setting up a warning triangle, I drive around both semis on the shoulder, over the wet grass, slowly, and on into the fog toward Yurga. From Yurga a commuter train leaves for Tomsk at 6:10. I leave the car by the station with the key in the ignition.

## После C
At the sign "Yurga 14" I turn onto a dirt road through the birches. I drive the car into a hollow, into the bushes, so it can't be seen from the road. I take the box under my arm and go on foot.

Fourteen kilometers across a soggy field in the fog. My shoes are soaked in the first kilometer; by the fifth I can't feel my toes. My knees, which spent seventeen years freezing on a bed, ache at every step. At dawn, muddy to the waist, I come out at the station in Yurga and get on the commuter train to Tomsk, in the last car. I sleep standing in the vestibule, leaning against the door.

## После D
I remember a gray tarp across the road and a beam in front of my face. I remember the box on the seat banging against the glove compartment.

At one forty-one I slow to thirty and turn on the hazards. A minute later a gray wall slowly grows out of the fog. The semi is standing across both lanes, its tarp wet, with the letters "…TRANS" on it. I stop five meters from it and sit for a long time with my hands on the wheel. Then I drive around it on the shoulder, over the wet grass. I reach Yurga at half past four and catch the first commuter train to Tomsk.

## После E
I remember the red lights disappearing from the mirror and how many minutes I drove alone through the white. So the oncoming lane is empty. I pull out from behind the semi and pass it at a hundred and twenty, blind, counting roadside posts. At one forty I slam the brakes, a kilometer before the gray wall, and the car slides sideways on the wet asphalt. I stop so close that the headlights are pressing into the letters "…TRANS."

I drive around it on the shoulder, over the grass, and floor it again. The semi I passed stays behind in the fog, and I don't know whether its driver will manage to brake. The Choir is silent about that, and I don't look back. I reach Yurga a little after four.
