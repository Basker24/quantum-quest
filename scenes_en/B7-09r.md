I tell Varya I'll drive with her as far as Novosibirsk, I've got business there. I don't have any business there. She's in her red Lada, I'm in mine, in front, and I keep exactly one minute between us. I measure it by the roadside posts: a little over a kilometer. My phone is in my pocket on speaker; she can hear it.

I know how this works. If I die on this road, I'll wake up a minute before my death and remember what killed me. And a minute later she'll be in that spot. So every death of mine is a trouble of hers that I'll see first and have time to name over the phone. Nobody has ever done this, except maybe Granddad, and not for a daughter.

The highway is wet, the semis run one after another like a freight train. Ahead is the long straight before Yurga, the same one where I once drove behind a semi with Varya and Kolka in the back seat. After that, the turn-off for Taiga, the town, where moose-crossing signs stand along the shoulders, and in Yurga a gas station where everybody stops for coffee.

Varya will drive behind me exactly the way I drive. If I want to know what's waiting for her here, it has to happen to me first.

## После D
On the straight before Yurga I call her: "Varya, drop to seventy and don't pass anybody, stay behind me." She grumbles but slows down. A minute later a small dark car with no headlights comes over the rise in the oncoming lane, and I let it go by, tucked in tight behind a semi. Varya says afterward that she saw something dark rush past her, and that her hands are still shaking.

We reach Akademgorodok by lunchtime. I sit in the car outside her dorm and look at my hands. They're not shaking. That scares me more than anything.

## После E
The whole way I talk to her on the phone, like a dispatcher talking a train through. Don't pass here, even if it's empty. Slow to sixty here, there are moose. In Yurga don't turn in at the gas station; I poured you coffee in a thermos, it's on your back seat. Varya does what I say and laughs.

"Dad, it's like you've already been there," she says.

"I have," I say. "A minute ago."

She gets to Akademgorodok without a scratch and texts me: "Made it. You're scary." I drive back alone, slowly. That night I lie looking at the green dot of the detector on the ceiling. I died three times in one morning, and I want more.
