I pull off my jacket, twist it into a rope and jam it into the neck over the lid, tight, like a cork in a bottle. The hissing stops. The mist at my feet stops rising, and I even laugh out loud, alone in an iron box.

The flask is silent for about forty seconds. Then something inside it hums, low, like pipes in winter, and its fat side goes ice-cold under my palm. The nitrogen inside keeps boiling and won't stop; it has nowhere to go. The jacket blows out along with the lid, with a bang that makes my ears pop, and a white cloud hits me in the face.

I fall on my back. The cloud settles on top of me, heavy and soft, and there's nothing to breathe under it. The last thing I see is my jacket sleeve on the ceiling; it's caught on the grille and hangs there.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing in the elevator between the second and third floors again. The bulb is flickering. The flask's lid is hissing, and my jacket is on me.

I remember the flask humming under my palm before it fired. The cold was coming from the neck, that's true. But you can't plug it. The nitrogen in it is boiling all the time, the vapor is looking for a way out, and if there's no way out, it makes one itself, lid and all.

I remember everything right; I just drew a stupid conclusion, like at a colloquium when you know the formula and plug in the wrong thing. Just in case, I zip my jacket up to my throat.
