I look left, Mom's way. To the left it's empty, the avenue white and clean all the way to the bridge. I go.

Light hits my right cheek before the sound does. I turn my head and see a bus, number seven, yellow windows, empty inside, very close; it's coming down the left lane, my lane, from where nobody has ever driven. The driver behind the glass opens his mouth. The brakes shriek on the snow, the bus swings sideways, and sideways is how it finds me.

I'm lying in the snow by the curb, looking at the display at the bus stop. 04:12. Snow falls on my face and doesn't melt. The driver kneels over me shouting the address into his phone. It smells of diesel, and under it of coal dust, Mom's hair, from when she held my hand at this very crossing.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing at the edge of the sidewalk by the clinic again, and the display at the stop shows 04:10.

My right cheek burns as if I'd been slapped. I remember the yellow windows, the empty bus, the driver with his mouth open. I remember the bus coming at me down the left lane, and I still had time to think it was driving wrong.

It was driving right. We drive on the left here now, and probably always have, as long as this world remembers itself.
