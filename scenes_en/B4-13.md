From the terrace of my house at Yagunovsky you can see the whole dry riverbed all the way to Kemerovo, and by Krasnaya Gorka, under the left bank, a black dot. I bought this house for that view. Every evening on TV scientists and priests take turns talking about the backwater.

The comet hangs over the riverbed. Galina Ivanovna, our astronomy teacher, promised it for the twenty-eighth of July 2061. We're seventy-two now, and we made it, almost all of us.

::if F_ПАРТНЁР == ЛЕРА
Lera is asleep. She's sixty-nine; she still shuffles a deck in the evenings, by herself, so her hands won't forget. The scar on her hand has gone completely white. The wild roses by the porch have grown so thick you have to walk to the door sideways.
::end

::if F_ПАРТНЁР != ЛЕРА
The house is dark. Six bedrooms, furniture under sheets in five. I live in the sixth and on the terrace.
::end

The Wager has been going on for six years, since '55, somewhere beyond the Urals and beyond the seas, among those whom the second chance told they won't die. Here it arrives as draft notices, news bulletins and the price of bread. Shadrin sold the open-pit mines to the ones doing the fighting back in '54. Shadrin always sells ahead of time.

At ten in the evening he calls himself. He's a hundred and thirteen. His voice is thin as a whistle and perfectly clear.

"Andrei Nikolayevich. Drive me to the backwater. I want to dive. I've been told that at four-twelve it's possible there. Your grandfather said it was possible there. I've been waiting since '86 for the water to go. My wheelchair has a little motor, but it won't go over the rocks. You owe me, I believe, or perhaps not; I no longer remember who owes whom. Drive me."

"Come to where it started," he says. "To the snag. I'm going down there tonight, even if it's on my backside. We'll talk. Fifty years we haven't really talked, and tonight we can. I stayed in that pit back then, Andryukh. Five hours. And you just keep climbing out and climbing out."

I put the phone on the table next to the storm jacket. The storm jacket has been lying on the wicker chair for two months. I haven't worn it, just moved it from place to place. The comet over the riverbed shines so brightly the terrace railing casts a shadow on the floor.

> Two calls in one evening. In seventy-two years nobody called me that often, except the ones who needed me dead.

## После A
I pick him up at the clinic at Pritomsky at half past one. Shadrin sits in his wheelchair by the entrance, dressed as if for a board meeting: gray suit, tie, a blanket over his knees. A nurse holds an umbrella over him, though it isn't raining. I load the wheelchair into the trunk myself and carry Shadrin to the back seat in my arms. He's light, like Sonka at five.

::drift ДРЕЙФ >= 10
While the nurse folds the umbrella, I look at the clinic doors. For twelve years there was a lyre on them. Now a harp is painted on the glass, golden, life-size, and under it the same word, "Orpheus." The nurse catches my look. "Pretty, isn't it? It's been here as long as I've worked here."
::end

"Thank you," he says in the car. "I knew you'd come. You always come, Andrei Nikolayevich. It runs in your family."

All the way to Krasnaya Gorka he's silent, looking out the window at the dry riverbed. Once, by the bridge, he asks, "The water will be cold, do you think?" I say it will be cold. He nods, pleased, as if that were good news.

## После B
"No," I tell Shadrin. "On your own, Leonid Arkadyevich."

He's silent on the line for so long that I think the connection has dropped. Then he says, "Well, on my own, then," and laughs, a whistle. And hangs up first, as always.

I put on the storm jacket. It's heavy, canvas, and smells of the trunk and a little of the river. I drive to Krasnaya Gorka, and from there I walk, over the rocks, to where Vitka is. The night fog over the riverbed smells of a damp root cellar, though there's been no water here for a long time.

## После C
I switch off the phone and put it in the desk drawer, with the bills. I put on the storm jacket. It's heavy, canvas, smells of the trunk and a little of the river, and sits on my shoulders as if it had been made for me.

::if F_ПАРТНЁР == ЛЕРА
I leave Lera a note in the kitchen, under the sugar bowl: "Gone fishing. I'll be back." I write it and look at the words "I'll be back" until the tea in my mug goes cold.
::end

I take a taxi to Krasnaya Gorka. The driver is young, with that particular face. The whole way he tells me he's had the second chance since he was seventeen, that he's crashed his motorcycle three times and it didn't do a thing to him. I listen and don't tell him how many times I've crashed.
