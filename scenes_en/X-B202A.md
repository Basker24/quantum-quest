I press the call button. The speaker crackles, and a woman's voice says, "The mechanic's on his way, wait, don't panic." I'm not panicking; I stand and wait, and the mist rises to my knees. I want to sit down, and I do; it's more comfortable. The last thing I notice is that I'm not scared at all.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing in the elevator between the second and third floors again. The bulb is flickering. The flask's lid is hissing.

I remember sitting on the floor, leaning against the wall, not scared, even cozy, like on a bus in winter by the heater. That's what's making me shake now. I died and didn't even notice.

The mist is creeping across the floor again, low, right at my sneakers. I don't feel like sitting down anymore.

The mist lay on the floor, and I sat right down in it, like in a bath. The cold is heavy; it runs down and collects there, and up under the ceiling, by the grille, it's warm and smells of dust. I lift my face and feel it on my cheek already, right now.
