I turned sixty in February, no cake and no guests. My knees creak in the mornings like the steps in the dorm on Lenin Avenue; on the stairs I hold the railing, though as recently as spring I was running up to the third floor two steps at a time. The body ages, however many times I die. The Choir doesn't ask about that.

::if F_ТИМУР
On the twenty-fourth of March, as every year, I buy two carnations and go to Kedrovka, to Timur. He's stayed twelve. His mother left long ago, and nobody visits him but me. I stand by the headstone and think that the ice could have been played again. Only the ice was Timur's, and Timur had no rewind.
::end

In September a new girl joins Sixth Grade V. Dasha Vesnina, twelve years old, family from Novosibirsk, her father something big at a coal company. She sits alone at the last desk with headphones on and spends the whole lesson drawing circles in her notebook, one inside another, like on water.

After class she comes up to my desk and says, without taking off her headphones:

"You sound in my head like a radio, only quieter."

I find out everything in one evening, because her mother cries and tells me herself. At nine Dasha drowned in the pool at their dacha, three minutes underwater. They brought her back and, "just in case," bought her a "second chance." The procedure was done in Novosibirsk, at the Orpheus clinic. Since then she hears voices and hardly sleeps. In the spring they're calling her back to the clinic, "for an examination."

## После A
On Tuesdays after classes we sit in the empty physics classroom. I tell her what I know myself. You don't drown the voices out; you learn to tell them apart. The one who calls you isn't always right. At four-twelve you don't have to get up; you can lie there and listen.

Two months later Dasha sleeps through the night for the first time. Her mother brings me a jar of blackcurrant jam, exactly like the one Mom traded for my gladioli in '96.

::if lived(В5-05)
Once Dasha looks up from her circles and asks, "Is it true you used to teach people not to drown? In warm water, where the fish are as big as a person. I dream about you with a yellow hose in your mouth." I put the kettle on and wait a long time for it to boil.
::end

## После B
"I'm a physics teacher," I tell Dasha's mother. "You need a good doctor."

Dasha looks at me from behind her mother's back as if I've switched off the one radio that ever picked anything up for her. At the end of the term she's moved to a private school. Sometimes I still hear her in the Choir, quiet and far away.

## После C
I tell Dasha's parents what I have no right to tell them. I know how this works, and at the clinic they won't help her; they'll study her. Dasha's father gives me a long, heavy look, like a section boss sizing up a new face worker. Then he says, "We'll see in the spring."
