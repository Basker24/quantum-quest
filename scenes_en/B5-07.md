In '16 the Blue Crab closed; the English owner sold it to some Chinese buyers for a hotel. I open my own place. A shed on stilts in Chalok, twenty tanks, a boat on credit from Ploy's uncle and a sign Ploy painted with a brush on a board: "Nam Un Diving." In Thai "nam un" means warm water. "Russians like warm water," Ploy explains. There are no Russians yet, but the sign is up.

::if F_ПАРТНЁР == ПЛОЙ
Ploy has been my wife for two years. In the office she's the boss, and I just dive. In the evenings she counts the money on a calculator and says we'll have a baby when the calculator stops showing a minus. For now the calculator shows a minus.
::end

::if F_ПАРТНЁР != ПЛОЙ && СВЯЗЬ_ПАРТНЁР >= 10
Ploy is my partner, half and half. She has no ring, and she's never once asked about it. Sometimes she looks at me across the counter as if she's waiting for me to say something important, and I talk about tanks.
::end

::if F_ПАРТНЁР != ПЛОЙ && СВЯЗЬ_ПАРТНЁР < 10
Ploy works at a big center on Sairee now, for some Koreans. She painted the "Nam Un" sign for me all the same, one evening, silently, and left. I dive alone, with the people she sends me.

The worst problem is air. A compressor of your own costs as much as a used Toyota. Getting tanks filled elsewhere eats a third of the takings. Across the road from us lives Somchai, sixty, in a Liverpool shirt with a cigarette, who has a six-room guesthouse and an old compressor in his shed, a German one, from the nineties. Somchai fills our tanks at half price.

"Same air," says Somchai, and laughs. "Thai air, cheap air."

His compressor stands next to the diesel generator that powers the guesthouse. The generator's exhaust pipe goes out one window of the shed, and the compressor's air intake out the next window. The air in Somchai's tanks is slightly sweet; you can taste it on the first breath. Last week a student from Shanghai complained of a headache after her second dive. Somchai said, "Sun. Too much sun."

> Somchai died in '23, of his heart, right on his own porch. Some Germans bought the compressor after him and, they say, were surprised for a long time.

## После A
We fill up at Somchai's. For the first time the calculator shows a plus, a small one, two thousand baht. In the evening Ploy comes in, looks at the number and puts a bottle of Chang and two glasses on the table.

Somchai's air is sweet, and after a month I don't notice it anymore. Sometimes my head aches after the second dive, toward evening, in the temples. Somchai says it's the sun. Ploy says I should drink less. I drink less, and my head aches anyway.

## После B
The bank in Surat Thani gives us a loan at such a rate that Ploy translates it into Russian for me in three words, all three of them obscene. The compressor arrives on the ferry a month later, Italian, new, in its factory wrap. I set it up in the shed, away from the road and the exhaust.

The air from it is nothing at all, no taste, no smell, the way it should be. For two months Ploy barely speaks to me, then brings me lunch on the boat and says, "Okay. Good machine." We'll be paying for it for six years.

## После C
I take the sample to Samui in a steel canister off a tank, on the ferry, on my knees. There's only one lab on Samui, at the hospital, and it takes a week to get the result. A week later they call me and say in English, slowly: carbon monoxide in the air is one and a half times over the limit. "Where is your intake? Near engine?"

Somchai takes offense at first, then smokes for a long time, then moves the air intake to the roof of the shed, on a long pipe, away from the generator. I buy him a filter with my own money. His air becomes tasteless. "Russian is crazy," Somchai tells all the neighbors, and keeps filling our tanks at half price.

## После D
We hold the wedding in May, at the temple near her mother's, with monks, white string on our wrists and chickens under the table. The compressor money goes on the wedding and on the debt to her uncle for the boat, and Ploy says that's how it should be.

::if F_В504 == МЭ
Ploy's mother cries all evening and tells everyone I'm the son from the roof. What that means she explains to the guests at length, with chickens.
::end

::if F_В504 == МОСТ
Ploy's mother comes up to me at the end of the evening, takes my hand and says in Thai, slowly, so I'll understand: "Don't sit on the bridge anymore. Go right away."
::end

We fill up at Somchai's. His air is sweet, and after a month I don't notice it anymore. Sometimes my head aches after the second dive, toward evening. I send Mom the wedding photo on Odnoklassniki. Mom answers: "Skinny. Feed her."
