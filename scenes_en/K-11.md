They love me. Varya calls every Sunday, Kolka brings his son for the whole summer, Andryusha climbs onto my knees even though he's seven already. They love the one who was always with them, who in their memory knocked his wife down at the rink and fought the teacher so Varya wouldn't be retrained to her right hand. I don't know that man. I wear his clothes, and they fit me.

Two years ago I read in Liza's printout that Granddad died at four-twelve. Now I think maybe he didn't die, but went off to where no one was waiting for him. Back then, over the printout, it seemed to me that this would never happen to me.

I write on a sheet of paper: "Andryusha, I've gone. Catch fish without me. Grandpa." I put the sheet on his boots. I close the door quietly, two turns, and leave the key under the mat, the way Mom used to.

The yard smells of dust and dry grass. My legs turn by themselves toward the river, toward where, beyond the five-story blocks, the Black Backwater stands in the dry riverbed. I turn around and walk the other way, to Sovetsky, to the station. The pull is so strong that I walk leaning forward, as into a wind, though there's no wind.

The station is empty. The screen on the platform has faded to gray; on it a gray-haired man in a good coat, "Orpheus," "second chance." They forgot to switch it off after the Wager, and it's been shining on the rails for four years. The first commuter train to Topki leaves at four-fifteen. I buy a ticket from the machine, to the end of the line, and sit by the window. There's no one in the car but me.

The comet hangs over the station roof, its tail pointing exactly at the river. At two minutes to, the roar in my head rises to a ringing, all the voices at once. Whether they're calling or waiting I can no longer tell. At four-twelve they all go silent together. For the first time since I was seven my head is quiet.

The train starts gently, without a jolt. I look at my hands on my knees and don't realize at once why I can see the ticket through them. The scar on my palm fades along with the palm. A voice runs through the car, a woman's, recorded many years ago:
