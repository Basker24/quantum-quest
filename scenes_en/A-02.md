The water in the backwater is warm, noon water. Somewhere in the seam, between night and noon, the bracelet slips off my wrist, and I don't manage to catch it. Time in the seam, it seems, doesn't run: the hands there stop at four-twelve again. I come out from under the snag in 1996, and the sun hits my eyes so hard I'm blinded.

The boy is lying on the bottom, on the sand, not deep at all. I grab him under the arms and drag him toward the bank. The storm jacket fills with water and pulls me down, my knees won't bend, and the boy is light, like a wet doll.

I lay the boy on the sand, on his side, then on his back. My hands count out thirty compressions and two breaths on their own. I was taught this in another life, or maybe in all of them at once. Beside us on her knees is a girl of about seven, panama hat in her fist, lips blue, counting out loud. A little way off a freckled boy of about eight stands frozen.

"One hundred ninety," she says. "One hundred ninety-one."

I press on the boy's chest with both hands and see my palms. Old, spotted. On the right, a long scar from the thumb to the wrist.

On the boy's palm is a fresh cut, in exactly the same place. The blood coming from it is pink with water. In the evening they'll put four stitches in it, and in the morning Nurse Zina will grumble that his veins are thin as threads.

I need to say something to him. He can't hear me; he's down there, in the warm close darkness. But I remember that back then, in the dark, I wasn't alone down there.

## После A
"Whose kid are you?" I ask him, though he can't answer.

And I answer for him myself, in a whisper:

"A Samokhin. So go back to your own."

I press on his chest, hard, like a ball in the stomach.

## После B
"Two hundred," I say along with her. "Two hundred one."

The two of us count, and I hear them counting with us, all of mine, from the fog over the water, thin, low, hoarse. We really, really need to not lose count.

## После C
I wipe my eyes with my palm. Scar outward, so he'll see it if he's looking down right now from his darkness.

Let him remember it; it'll come in handy.

## После D
I lay my palm on his forehead. My palm is big, rough and warm, not Mom's at all.

"Mom's right here," I say. "Don't be scared."
