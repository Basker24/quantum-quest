I lean on the jackhammer, and the seam breathes out in my face. The crack opens all at once, from floor to roof, and coal comes out of it. It pours like water from a burst pipe, black, loose and heavy, hits my knees, my chest, and knocks me backward.

I'm carried backward down the face, turned over, and I can't tell up from down anymore. Coal gets into my mouth, my nose, my ears, fine and cold. Just like the water in the backwater, only this presses in from all sides, tighter with every shove. I try to breathe once, and it crunches in my mouth.

My lamp is shining somewhere under my arm, in the black, a yellow spot. I think, "Oh, come on. Again." Then I smell Mom's hair.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing at the face again with the jackhammer in my hands. The seam is crackling, and Mikhalych is listening to it with his head cocked.

Something crunches between my teeth. I run my tongue over my gums, and there's nothing there; they're clean. My hands grip the jackhammer so hard my fingers are white.

The crack above the jackhammer is thin as a hair. In a minute it will open from floor to roof, and I'll be flying backward down the face with a mouthful of coal. How I know this I don't understand. But the taste of that minute is still in my mouth.

The seam above the stall clicks, short, like a sunflower seed on a frying pan. It clicked like that then too, and I stood right here, in the stall, right by the face, counting the clicks.
