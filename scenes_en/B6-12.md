I get through Novokuznetsk in three days. The steel plant stands idle, its stacks smokeless, rusty as nails in an old board. People live in the city, but quietly; there are more old people on the streets than cars. At the bus stops hang screens advertising the "second chance," half of them smashed. Outside a shop two men argue about what year the Wager ended, and each remembers his own year. I don't interfere. Maybe each of them really did have his own.

Below the city the Tom moves away from its old bed. The water in it is knee-deep, and between me and the water lie the steel plant's settling ponds. A kilometer of black field, flat, cracked into hexagons like Granny Nyura's heels. The crust on top is dry, gray with dust, but in the cracks something glistens.

Across the field, on concrete supports, runs an old pipe, rusty, a meter thick. To the right, along the ponds, runs an earthen dike, but it goes a long way around, right to the forest, eight kilometers or so.

In the hole it bubbles, slowly, like porridge, and smells of rotten eggs.

## После A
I walk fast, without stopping, like on ice in March. The crust underfoot springs and crunches; in one place it sags, and I step over without looking. In the cracks right by my boots something sighs. I don't look down.

I come out at the ford twenty minutes later, soaked with sweat. I sit down in the water fully dressed, up to my waist, and sit for a long time washing my boots. The water is warm and murky. Downstream, on a rock, sits a boy with a fishing rod, looking at me like a ghost.

"You came across the ponds?" he asks. "People drown there."

I say I know.

## После B
I close my eyes and walk toward where it's just quiet. The Choir leads me along the pipe, to the right of it, about twenty steps off. When I drift to the left, the back of my head goes cold, like Dad's at the longwall, and I turn.

::if F_В611 == ПЛОТ
I leave the raft on the shoal. There's no dragging it across the field, and they say there's a ferry at the ford.
::end

The crust underfoot is hard, dry, cracked. To the left, two steps away, I see bubbles in a hole and go around them. I come out at the ford half an hour later and only there open my eyes.

## После C
I drop everything heavy by the pipe: the pot, the hatchet, my spare boots.

::if F_В611 == ПЛОТ
And the raft. Six blue barrels stay lying on the shoal, and the river slowly goes around them.

The dike runs through full sun, without a single tree. At the third kilometer the water in my flask runs out. At the sixth my vision goes dark, and I sit on the slope until it passes. By evening I come out at the ford through the forest, and my legs won't bend. On the bank, by a campfire, sit people who are also going down for the comet. They give me tea and ask nothing.

## После D
I remember how the crust crunched and how warm my legs were, before everything stopped smelling of anything. I climb a support of the pipe, up rusty rungs, and stand on the pipe. It's round, hot from the sun, a meter wide.

::if F_В611 == ПЛОТ
The raft stays on the shoal. I untie the rope and put it in my backpack, in case it comes in handy.
::end

I walk along the pipe for half an hour, arms out, like on a log across a creek when I was a kid. Below, in the holes in the crust, something gurgles and sighs. In one place the pipe has rotted through, and I crawl over the hole on all fours. At the far edge of the field I climb down a support and stand for a long time holding on to it until my knees stop shaking.
