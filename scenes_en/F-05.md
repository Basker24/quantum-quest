In the willows, while the seam still holds, I open the Choir, only not to myself. All my life I listened to it; now I give it away. My dead brothers and all the copies of my tuning fork, the Moscow ones, the Dubai ones, the Novosibirsk ones, I release at once, to the whole Earth.

The seam holds for one more minute, and that minute is enough. Every person on the planet remembers how they died in a neighboring world. Some fell down the stairs, some drowned, some didn't wake up. Later, when they compare notes, it will turn out that for everyone it happened in the same minute, at four-fifteen Kemerovo time.

At four-sixteen I stop being, and it doesn't hurt at all.

In the morning, at the bus stop on Sovetsky, a bus driver sits on the curb crying, his face in his hands. His passengers stand around him and don't hurry him.

At a border post a man in uniform lays his rifle on the parapet and looks at it for a long time, as if it belonged to someone else.

At the Orpheus clinic in Novosibirsk, on Krasny Avenue, the screens go dark. Those who bought themselves a "second chance" learn for the first time what it's made of and who paid for it. They say many of them called their mothers that morning.

Sonka calls a brother she no longer has and listens to the ringing for a long time.

People who for one morning remembered their own deaths walk carefully for a long time afterward, as on March ice. All summer in Kemerovo almost nobody runs a red light, and the traffic police on Sovetsky are bored.

! Ending "The Morning After." The Choir opened to everyone. My Life shows what other roads led to this morning.
