::if F_Т02 == ЧЕЙ
The voice in the dark is a kid's voice, a voice from somebody's yard.

"Whose kid are you?"

"I'm a Samokhin," I say, even though I don't have a mouth down here.

"So go back to your own," says the voice, and shoves me in the chest. It hurts, like a ball in the stomach.
::end

::if F_Т02 == ХОР
I listen. The whisper comes apart into voices, and they all talk in my voice, only one is thinner, one is lower, and one wheezes like Dad after a shift. They're counting along with the girl up above: one hundred ninety, one hundred ninety-one. They really, really need to not lose count.
::end

::if F_Т02 == ЛИЦО
I look down and see myself. I'm lying on the sand, as white as that snag, and an old man in a wet striped sailor's shirt is kneeling over me and pushing on my chest with both hands. His face is like Dad's, only old and unshaven. He wipes his eyes with his palm, and there's an old scar on the palm.
::end

::if F_Т02 == МАМА
I call for Mom, and the dark smells of coal, of Mom's hair after a shift. Somebody puts a big rough palm on my forehead. It isn't Mom's, but I'm not scared anyway.
::end

Then something hits me in the chest like a horse's hoof, and I cough. Half the river pours out of my mouth. Sand in my teeth, sun, the sky too bright. Somebody turns me on my side and holds me by the shoulder with a heavy wet hand, and the water keeps coming and coming out of me. Then the hand on my shoulder is gone.

By the time I can see, whoever pumped the water out of me is gone too. Somebody has covered my legs with a wet canvas storm jacket, and bare footprints lead off into the willows, long ones, a grown-up's. The sand in them is still dark with water, and the bushes are swaying, though there's no wind.

Nika is squatting next to me, lips blue, panama hat in her fist, looking at me like I'm somebody else.

::if !one_life()
"Two hundred and fifty-two," she says. "I counted. Two hundred and fifty-two seconds."
::end

I open my fist. On top of the fresh cut lies a man's watch on a metal bracelet, wet, with a little star on the dial. The hands are stopped at four-twelve. Vitka leans in to look, and I don't know why, but I close my fist again.
::end

::if F_Т01 == ДОЛГ

::end

::if F_Т01 == БУБЛИК

Half an hour later the ambulance comes: an old UAZ van with a red cross and one headlight missing, the kind everybody calls a "loaf," because that's what it looks like. Mom flies into it still in her work overalls, slaps my face, and then hugs me so hard I can't breathe again. At the hospital they sew up my palm, four stitches. In the hallway the doctor says "hypothermia" and "lucky" to Mom. Until evening the police go around Rudnichny, our end of town, asking about the old man in the striped shirt. Nobody knows anyone like that.

The next day another doctor comes into the ward, not one of ours. He's in a gray suit, no white coat, with a briefcase, and he says he's from Tomsk, "sharing experience." He talks quietly and keeps rubbing his eyes. He takes my blood, two test tubes, even though they already took some in the morning and Nurse Zina grumbled that my veins are thin as threads. His fingers shake, but he gets the vein on the first try, not like Zina. Then he leans in really close, so Zina can't hear, and asks:

"What did you hear down there?"

::if F_Т02 == ХОР
I tell him they were counting. He sits down on my bed right away, even though you're not allowed to sit on the beds; Zina chases people off for it. He sits there all white. I think maybe they're going to sew up his palm too.
::end

::if F_Т02 != ХОР
I don't know what to say, so I shrug. He nods like I answered him anyway, ruffles my hair and leaves. He puts the test tubes in his briefcase.
::end

> That "doctor" was forty. Looked a good sixty. In ninety-six everybody did.

At night I lie holding my stitched-up palm on top of the blanket so it won't get wet. I sleep badly, and I keep wondering how that man from Tomsk knew you could hear anything down there under the water.
