They bring my shift up at ten at night. Dad stays on for a second shift, covering for Kolya Yeryomenko, whose wife is giving birth. In the bathhouse I've just soaped my head when the lights go out. Then they come back on, and the floor under my feet jolts, short, like a train car going over a rail joint. Every man in the bathhouse goes quiet at once, washcloths in hand.

At 23:20 methane explodes at the Glubokaya mine. There are over three hundred men below, and Dad is one of them. I find this out ten minutes later, in wet shorts and a jacket over my bare skin, outside the dispatcher's office, where every phone is ringing at once and nobody picks up.

By midnight the buses from the paramilitary mine rescue unit are lined up by the shaft. The rescuers are guys in orange, with breathing-apparatus boxes on their backs. The squad leader, gray-haired Palych, with a voice like it's coming out of a barrel, is lining his men up by the cage.

::if F_Т08 == ПАЛЫЧ
It's the same Palych I hauled air cylinders for, three nights in '05. He recognizes me, nods and says, "Grown up, Lucky Junior." And turns back to his men.
::end

The first squad goes down at half past midnight. Men come out of the mine on their own, one at a time and in groups, black, with white eyes, and at the lamp room Tanya checks off tags in the log and doesn't raise her head. Dad isn't among them. Down below, they say, longwall five is on fire, and the gas keeps coming.

A crowd gathers at the cage. Men from my shift, from the neighboring sections, some in work overalls, some in regular clothes; they need to go down, everyone has somebody down there. Vitka is here too, in slippers on bare feet, holding the shift foreman by the sleeve and saying something to him, close, right in his face.

::if F_Т08 == ПАЛЫЧ
I remember what Palych said in '05, on the third night, when I was falling asleep on the bench. "Where there's fire and the gas keeps coming, it'll blow a second time. We wait four hours. Then we go."
::end

::if F_Т08 != ПАЛЫЧ && РАЗ >= 40
In our safety course they told us about secondary explosions. If there's a fire below and gas is still coming out, the next explosion is only a matter of time. The lecturer repeated it three times, and half the group was asleep.
::end

::if F_ГИПОКСИЯ
My head is buzzing over my left eyebrow, like in March, when I lay face down in a puddle. I rub my eyebrow with a finger and can't stop.
::end

The cage comes up empty and stands with its door open. It smells of smoke and warm iron.

## После A
I jump into the cage with the second squad. Palych looks at me but doesn't throw me out; he has no time for me. Down at the shaft bottom it smells of burning, and from the drift that leads to longwall five comes a warm wind, like from a stairwell where the garbage chute is on fire.

::if F_Т08 == ПАЛЫЧ
That's when I remember his "four hours," and my feet stop on their own. I stay at the shaft bottom and haul the men who come out of the drifts on their own to the cage. Palych goes into the smoke with his men.
::end

::if F_Т08 != ПАЛЫЧ
That's when it hits me that the gas is still coming out down there and the fire is still burning, and I stop dead. I don't go any farther. I stay at the shaft bottom and haul the men who come out of the drifts on their own to the cage.
::end

At 3:50 the drift breathes out fire. A wave of hot air knocks me off my feet and rolls me along the rails toward the shaft, and I lie with my arms over my head until the roaring stops. Later three men from the second squad come out of the smoke, and Palych comes last, his mask ripped loose around his neck. The others don't come out.

Dad is brought up after seven in the morning, through the ventilation shaft at the far end of the field. At home he doesn't lie down. He goes to the china cabinet, takes the envelope with the wax seal marked "For Kolya" out from behind the crystal, where Mom put it ten years ago, and breaks the seal over the kitchen table. Inside is a single sheet, blue ink, Granddad's handwriting. He reads it to himself for a long time, then hands it to me.

"Kolya. If you're reading this, I didn't come back. You're lucky, son, and it isn't luck. I passed it on to you without meaning to, and forgive me for that. Don't go below two hundred. And if you have a son, keep him away from the water." At the bottom, a date: January '86.

"Three times," says Dad. "In '05 I only had a feeling, Andryukha. But this time I remember. Like watching a movie about myself."

"Me too," I say. "Since November."

He nods, as if that's what he thought. Then he says he knows. His father didn't work at the mine, he worked under it, below every level. Grandma used to say Tisha went too deep, and Dad thought all his life she meant his work. In '86 there was gas down there, six men died, and Granddad was the seventh. They buried him in a closed coffin, poured concrete into the level and told everyone to forget. "I never went below two hundred," says Dad. "Twenty-four years at minus one-eighty. They kept trying to transfer me, and I kept refusing. Didn't know why myself."

## После B
I sit down on a rail by the shaft and wait. It's the hardest thing I've ever done. The second squad walks past me into the cage. I grab Palych by the sleeve and tell him about the second explosion, and he looks at me like a kid teaching him to tie his shoelaces. "I know," he says. "I've got men down there." And he goes down.

At 3:50 the ground shakes a second time, harder than the first. Dust blows up out of the shaft, and the cage isn't brought up for a long time. When it is, they carry out orange suits, and I count them, the way I once counted names on the board outside the pithead building. Palych isn't among them; he walks out on his own, last, and sits down on the ground by the cage without taking off his mask.

## После C
I stand in the cage door with my arms spread, like a goalkeeper. Vitka comes at me first, in his slippers, and I hold him until he stops pushing. Then Yeryomenko, whose wife is giving birth and whom Dad covered for. He punches me in the cheekbone, clumsily, and sits down on the ground himself. "Tikhonych is down there for me," he says. "For me." I hold him too.

At 3:50 the ground shakes a second time, harder than the first. Dust blows up out of the shaft, and everyone I held back is standing by the cage alive, looking down the shaft. Nobody tries to go anywhere anymore. Vitka puts his arm around my neck and says nothing; my cheekbone swells and stays yellow for a week.

## После D
I catch Palych at the cage by the strap of his breathing set.

"At 3:50 it'll blow a second time," I say. "In the drift to longwall five. I've been there."

He looks at me for a long time, and I can see him searching my eyes for vodka. He doesn't find any. "You've been there," he repeats, not as a question. Then he turns to his men and holds the second squad at the shaft bottom: "We wait. I said, we wait." The first squad is already down; no amount of shouting will reach them.

At 3:50 the drift breathes out fire. The second squad is standing by the shaft, and it only knocks them off their feet. The first squad doesn't come out. Palych comes up top, sits down next to me on a rail and says, looking at the ground, "You gave me back five today. But not six." I know that myself. I counted.
