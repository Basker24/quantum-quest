The window is open, and beyond the bars is the night, clear, July, with poplar fluff. I go to the bars, press my face to them and breathe in.

What I breathe in is smoke. There isn't any; the air outside is transparent; I can see the lamp by the gate and even the midges around it. But something hot and greasy is forcing its way into my throat, with burnt rubber and tar. My eyes sting. I cough, breathe in again, and again it's smoke, and with every breath there's more.

I grip the bars with both hands. They're warm and rough with paint. Somewhere close by something crackles, slate roofing bursts, someone behind a door that isn't here pounds on it and calls, "Andryukha!" My legs buckle, and I slide down the wall without letting go of the bars, and outside the fluff is still drifting and the lamp still burning.

The last to come is Mom. Her hair smells of coal dust, and I recognize that smell even through the smoke.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm sitting on the bed again, and the third one beyond the window is coughing, "Open the window."

I don't breathe. I count to twenty, to thirty, and only then breathe in, through my nose, carefully. Ward air, bleach, other people's socks, Semyonych's hangover breath. There's no smoke in it, but my throat is raw, as if I'd stood by a campfire half the night.

I suffocated at an open window. Clean wind was blowing in my face, and I suffocated on it. My fingers can still feel the paint on the bars, the little bumps, and I rub them against the sheet.
