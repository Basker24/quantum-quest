I jump, and the current takes me at once, the way you're picked up under the arms. The thirty meters to the villa turn out to be endless, because I'm swimming toward the villa and being carried sideways. The water is warm, murky, and everything is floating in it at once: boards, bags, flip-flops, somebody's fridge.

I'm dragged toward the breach. There the water spins in a funnel, and a palm tree spins in the funnel, roots up. I paddle against it with all the strength I have at fifty-six, and it isn't enough. My shoulders give out first, then my legs.

The funnel stands me upright and pulls me down, into the breach, into the concrete. I hit the edge of the dike with my knee, then my head. Underwater, rebar sticks out of the concrete, rusty, and my jacket catches on it, and I can't get free.

Up above, the villa's roof is glowing. Henk is standing there with his glass. He doesn't see me; he's watching the water like television. My air is running out. Underwater they're whispering again, many voices, close, and Mom's hair smells of coal, though where would there be coal in Bangkok.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing in the boat again, megaphone in hand, and Henk is raising his glass on the roof.

My knee hurts. I roll up my trouser leg, and the knee is whole, just old, with the lump I got back in Kemerovo at the skating rink.

I remember the funnel at the breach and the palm tree in it, roots up. I remember the rebar and how it held me by my jacket. And Henk stood on the roof and watched the water like television. He won't die, that one.
