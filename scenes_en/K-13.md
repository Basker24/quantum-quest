The door to the room isn't locked. The Muscovite in a white shirt is sitting on the bed, Lunyov is standing by the window with the box in his hands, and both look at me like a drunk who's got the wrong floor. I take the box out of Lunyov's hands; he doesn't hold on to it. I'm already at the door when the crew-cut one from the corridor comes in after me.

He doesn't shout or wave his arms. He just takes out from under his leather jacket what he was carrying there and shoots me in the back, once, in passing, the way you close a door behind you.

I fall on the stairs. The box falls beside me, the lid flies off, the thermos rolls down the steps and rings, intact. The step is cold, painted green. I wait to be thrown a minute back, to the corridor, to the sagging chair, so I can do it differently.

There's no minute back. The Choir left with the seam; I let it go myself. In 1998 I have one death, like everyone, and this is it.

Lunyov comes down, steps around me and picks up the thermos. The last thing I see is his shoes, worn down, scuffed at the toes, and how he wipes the thermos with his sleeve before carrying it back upstairs.

! Dead end "Ninety-Eight." In '96 there's no Choir: here you have one death. The blood went to Orpheus anyway. The strength that would have helped here belonged to the Welder, not to you.
