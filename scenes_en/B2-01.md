Tomsk greets me with rain and cabbage pies at the Tomsk-1 station, twelve rubles apiece. I eat three while I look for the right bus, and decide I like this city.

The dorm on Lenin Avenue is old, with stucco on the front and cockroaches in the kitchen. The woman at the front desk looks at my passport as if I'd drawn it myself. There are three of us in 412. Den from Seversk, the closed nuclear town, says everything in his town glows, and laughs louder than anyone at his own joke. Tagir from Kolpashevo silently lines his shelves with cans of stewed meat his mother gave him and shares with nobody. A week later he and I are eating them together, and Den is offended.

For the first six months nobody in the physics department says a word about stars; it's all calculus. At the first lecture the associate professor writes some integral on the board, turns around and says, "Half of you will be gone after the first exams. Don't take it personally. It's statistics." We laugh, and nobody believes him.

On the department noticeboard, between the timetable and a notice about potato-picking duty, hang photos of the faculty. Professor V. P. Lunyov looks out of his card with tired eyes. I've definitely seen him somewhere, and for half a day I can't remember where.

The Tom here is the same as at home, only farther downstream and wider. In the evenings I walk along the embankment and figure how far it is to swim from here to my backwater. It turns out it's no distance at all, because the water flows the other way.

## После A
I sit over Demidovich until two in the morning, and Den snores so loud the glass on the nightstand rattles. At the first colloquium the associate professor looks at my record book, then at me, and says, "Samokhin? From Kemerovo? Well, well." I pass my first exams with straight A's, and from that day on everybody who can be bothered asks to copy off me.

## После B
At night Tagir and I unload freight cars at the Tomsk-2 yard: cement, sugar, once a whole car of bananas. The foreman pays cash in an envelope and calls us "students" in the voice people usually swear in. By New Year's I have new sneakers, my shoulders are a size bigger, and I fall asleep in calculus lectures.

## После C
I fit into the dorm fast: guitar, the department comedy team, Baltika on the windowsill and talking till morning about everything in the world. I pass my exams with C's, on the second try. But by spring half the dorm knows me, and the woman at the front desk doesn't look at my passport anymore.

## После D
Almost every night I go down to the embankment. I sit on the parapet over the Tom and listen to the water rustle. Sometimes in that rustling I think I hear somebody counting, quietly, in two voices. I don't tell anybody, and each time I get back to the dorm when the windows have already gone dark.
