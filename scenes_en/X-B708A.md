I run straight, across the middle. About ten meters from the hole the ice under me doesn't crack, it just ends, and I go under at once, head and all, in my jacket and felt boots.

The cold squeezes my chest so I can't breathe out. My jacket fills with water and pulls me down. Under the ice it's light, gray-green, like inside a bottle. I push up and hit the top of my head on the ice. There's no hole above me; it's somewhere off to the side, and I can't tell where anymore.

I scrape at the ice from below with my nails and pound on it with my fist. Up above, a dark shape is running across the ice, somebody with a boat hook, and I pound for him, pound, until the air runs out and I breathe in water.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing by the fence again, shovel in my hands, and Kolka's head comes up over the water and disappears.

I remember the ice above my head from the inside, gray, with bubbles in it. I remember pounding on it with my fist. I open my fist, dry and warm, and my fingers won't do what I tell them.

I remember the ice didn't end at the hole, it ended earlier, in the middle. Where a cold spring comes up in the pond in summer.
