"Coming!" I shout, and go left, toward the voice that's closer.

"This way, this way," it says, glad and warm. "Here it is, the gap. Squeeze through sideways, it's narrow."

Under my hand is rock, wet, cold, and between two rocks there really is a gap. I squeeze into it sideways, my backpack catches, I yank it, and the rocks underfoot suddenly slide, all at once, with a sound like a bucket of gravel being poured out.

I fall on my back and slide down with the scree. I grab at rocks, and the rocks slide with me. The fog ends below; I shoot out of it, and beneath me opens the lake, small, green, far, far away, like a button at the bottom of a well. Air whistles in my ears. The voice beside me laughs, with my laugh, and says, "There. Now you know."

I feel the impact with my back and the back of my head, once. Then it smells of Mom's hair, coal dust, though the nearest mine is almost a thousand kilometers from here.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing on the wet scree in the fog again, and from the left they're calling, "Andryukha! Over here!"

My back burns from my shoulder blades to my tailbone, as if I'd been switched with birch twigs in a bathhouse. I gulp cold fog. My knees shake so hard the scree rustles underfoot.

He was laughing. He knew where he was leading me; he'd climbed in there himself, into his own gap, in Tomsk. He didn't call me out of malice. He was just bored down there alone.
