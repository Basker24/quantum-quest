Serafim died in November, in his sleep, a week before the river froze. He was seventy-seven. In the evening he was still splitting kindling, and in the morning I found him on the bench, on his side, with his hand under his cheek, the way children sleep. A death like that has no fork; the Choir can't help there. I buried him by the chapel.

::end

::if !F_КАМЕРТОН
I put his tuning fork in his coffin, in his hand. All his life he gave the first note with it; let him give it there too.

Since the spring of '16 there's been a new voice in the Choir. It doesn't shout or beg. It speaks in long sentences, calmly, with pauses, the way people speak at a lecture, and it answers my questions as if it had been expecting them. It calls itself "the one who studied in Tomsk." He died in April '16, at minus six hundred forty, under Krasnaya Gorka. His heart didn't restart.

From him I learned who Granddad was. Not just a choirmaster. Tikhon Savelyevich Samokhin, physicist, project "Tuning Fork," a laboratory underground, on the minus six hundred forty level, under that very Glubokaya where Dad cut coal for thirty years. Experiment forty-one, January '86, four-twelve. Granddad struck the tuning fork on himself back in '61, and died at the forty-first experiment. Through Granddad it passed to Dad, sleeping. Through Dad, to me.

"And the doctor from Tomsk," said the one who studied in Tomsk, "who took your blood at the hospital, two test tubes, and rubbed his eyes. That was Lunyov. Vadim Petrovich. Granddad's graduate student. Remember the name."

Since the summer before last, people have started coming to me. At first one at a time: a widow from Myski who hears her husband, a boy from Prokopyevsk who saw his own death in a dream. Then five at a time. This summer there are fifteen tents by the hermitage.

On Saturday Dad arrives by boat with the hunter. He's fifty-seven, retired, all gray. There's a bundle on his knees. He sits on the porch looking at the tents below and the cross by the chapel.

"Your gran died in March," he says. "Before she died she said to give you this. Granddad's, I think."

In the bundle is a canvas storm jacket, old, faded white at the seams, and a sailor's striped shirt.

## После A
In the morning I go down to the tents. They're waiting for me by the campfires, quietly, as if I were a doctor about to start office hours. The widow from Myski asks whether it's true her husband hears her. I say I don't know. The boy from Prokopyevsk asks whether he'll die the way he saw in his dream. I say if he saw it, then he already knows where not to stand. He thinks and nods.

By evening my voice is gone. I listen to them all day and say almost nothing, and they leave the campfires light, as if from a bathhouse. At night someone leaves a jar of honey on the porch with a note: "Thank you for not being a saint."

## После B
"Leave," I say by the campfires. "This isn't a circus. The dead won't answer you, and neither will I."

Not all of them leave. By autumn there are five tents instead of fifteen, but those five stay until the snow. People sit by the river in silence, not coming close, just looking at the hermitage the way you look at a church you're not allowed into.

In the evenings, in the quiet, the Choir sounds louder than ever. The one who studied in Tomsk tells me, "Right. What they need from you isn't what you can give." I don't argue with him. It doesn't make me feel any better.

## После C
For three nights I sit by the stove with a notebook and write while he dictates. Experiment forty-one. Cold, a screen, four-twelve. The tuning fork in your head, and how to make it ring longer. Half the words I don't understand; he repeats them patiently, like Nina Petrovna at the blackboard.

On the third night I ask him why he's telling me this.

"Because you'll listen to the end," he says. "I didn't. They told me 'twelve minutes,' and I lay down."

In the morning I burn half of what I wrote in the stove. The rest I hide in a tin tea caddy and bury under the porch.

## После D
"Dad," I say. "Your father was a physicist."

I tell him everything I know from the one who studied in Tomsk. The laboratory under the Glubokaya, the forty-first experiment, four-twelve. Dad listens without interrupting and smokes one cigarette after another.

"Minus six hundred forty," he says at the end. "I was at minus four hundred for twenty years. And he, it turns out, was right under me."

He strokes the storm jacket on his knees, for a long time, the way you stroke a dog. Then he says, "My mother knew. Knew all her life and kept quiet." In the morning he leaves with the hunter. He hugs me on the bank, for the first time since I was seven.
