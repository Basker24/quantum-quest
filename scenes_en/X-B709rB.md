At the Taiga turn-off I don't brake. Ninety, the way people drive in the morning when they're late. The moose-crossing sign flashes past as a white blur.

The moose comes out of the birch trees not from the side but right in front of me, all of it at once, tall as a garage door. My high beams in the drizzle stop at its legs, long gray legs, and its head is somewhere higher, above the roof. I stand on the brake, and the car still goes in under its belly.

It comes down on the roof. The roof folds down onto me, heavy and soft, and the cold fur smells of swamp. The last thing I see is its hoof twitching in front of my face, slowly, like a pendulum.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm coming up on the Taiga turn-off again, and the moose sign shows white in the drizzle.

I remember the gray legs in front of the hood and the hoof in front of my face. I remember the fur smelling of swamp, and the roof folding.

I slow to sixty. It comes out of the birches in the same place, huge, and stands on the shoulder. I go around it in the oncoming lane, slowly, and call Varya: "Slow down to sixty. Moose." Varya will be here in a minute, doing ninety.
