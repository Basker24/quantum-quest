The water really is like fresh milk. I wade in up to my waist, my chest, and lie back on it. The moon is right above me, the music from the shore muffled, as if through a wall, and it's so good lying on the water that I close my eyes.

Then red-hot wire settles across my chest. Several strands at once, from shoulder to belly, and another wraps around my arm. I jerk, the wire comes with me and burns harder. I scream and swallow water. The pain is such that I stop knowing where the shore is.

I reach the shore on all fours. How, I don't remember. Sand sticks to my chest, and my chest burns as if the skin had been peeled off. My heart beats once, then skips, then beats three times in a row, then goes quiet for a long time. I can't breathe in; my ribs won't obey.

People are dancing all around. Someone's foot in glow paint steps over me. A girl with a bucket leans down and shouts in my ear, "Are you okay?" I want to say "help me," but my lips won't move. She shrugs and goes on dancing.

Under the beat, through the beat, someone in my head is singing a lullaby. In Russian, quietly, in many voices, that one about the little gray wolf. The last thing is the smell of Mom's hair, coal dust from the processing plant. Here, on a Thai beach, there's nowhere for it to come from.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing at the water's edge again, the bucket on the sand, and Tolik is waving his arms at me from the water.

My chest burns. I pull up my T-shirt, and there's nothing there, skin like any skin, tanned, not a single welt. I rub it with my palm, and it hurts anyway.

I remember how the girl with the bucket stepped over me. I remember her foot in green paint right by my face. She asked if I was okay and went off to dance. Nobody noticed me. I lay on the sand among three thousand people and died for three minutes, and they danced.
