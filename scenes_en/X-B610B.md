I shove the sheepskin into the creek, and it barely gets wet: there's a palmful of water among the stones, warm, like a puddle after rain. I lie face down in it and cover myself with the coat, head and all. The stones under my belly are dry; water stands only in the hollows.

The fire comes faster than I thought. First my back gets hot, then the coat starts to smoke by itself, from inside, and under it there's nothing to breathe. I press my mouth to the wet moss in a hollow and draw air through it, and the air is hot, as from a stovepipe.

I throw off the coat when it catches fire. I can't get up anymore. The last thing I see is fire running over the stones above the creek, small and cheerful, and the water in the hollow by my face bubbling.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing by the cabin again, and rust-colored smoke is rising over the valley from below.

My back is burning, though my shirt is whole. I look at the creek. There's a palmful of water in it; it glints only in the hollows between the stones.

No one has come here since I walked away across the ice in Myski. If people came, someone would have built a little dam on the creek, the way they do by every cabin where a man doesn't live alone. Down, to the river, across yesterday's burn, the fire has nothing left to eat.
