I signed the report on the twenty-eighth of November '33, with a gold pen that a courier from Moscow later came to collect. The signature came out even, with a flourish. In January they gave me the Kuzbass directorate, and in spring they planted birches and a lawn on the clay above the spoil heap.

After that everything ran over level ground, and I signed. The mines in the region closed one after another, because nobody needed coal anymore, and each one closed on my paper. I signed the last liquidation in Kemerovo in '48, at the Severnaya mine, and at the banquet they gave me an engraved watch. That year the holding bought me a "second chance," like all the directors: one minute back for the price of a three-room apartment in the center. I had no use for it, but here it isn't done to refuse.

::if F_В101 == ТАНЯ
Tanya stopped picking up the phone in the spring of '34. The last thing she said to me was, "You brought it back, Samokhin. Only the wrong thing." I never understood what she meant, never got around to asking, and in '40 she moved to her daughter's in Tomsk.
::end

Vitka never spoke to me again. They didn't invite me to his funeral in '59, and I stood outside the fence at Kedrovka until Yegor came out to me and said, looking at his shoes, "He asked me to tell you the heap is still burning."

::if F_В122 != ОЖОГ
Vitka came to see me with a bottle three more times, the first year. Then he asked from the doorway, "Well, did you put it out?" I said, "On paper, yes." He didn't come again. They buried him in '59, at Kedrovka, and I stood by the coffin in a good coat, and Yegor gave me his hand without looking at me.
::end

My house is in Gorki, the front row, at the very top, with a terrace over the river. The floor in it is warm all year round, and the builders laugh that they needn't have put in heating. The lawn on the slope yellows in circles; they relay it every spring. Sometimes steam comes from a crack in the tiles by the pool, and the gardener hoses it down when I'm not looking.

About the Wager, to which in '55 generals with a bought "second chance" went off sure they wouldn't die, I read in reports, in the "supplies" column. Mine I never used.

> It all started with the bag from the Maria-Ra supermarket. Twenty-three surnames in a column, and the bag laid across them. Afterward I used to think that if I'd said "stop" back then, there'd have been no directorate and no terrace. I'd think it and close my eyes.
::end

Below the terrace, down in the white dry riverbed, the backwater shows black. From my height you can see the whole of it, like a coin on a palm. Over it is fog, over the fog the comet, and the comet's tail lies exactly on the water.

It has pulled me there all year, and this last week so hard that I've stopped sleeping. The whisper behind my ear, which has been with me since '09, has become a roar, like the assignment hall before a shift. I stand on the terrace in my bathrobe holding the glass railing with both hands. You can only get down from here to the river by the road, the long way around, forty minutes, or straight down the slope. Walking on the slope is forbidden. The sign "Danger! Burning spoil heap" was taken down in '34, on my orders.

At four o'clock I open the archive on my tablet and find the report. "Fire extinguished. Spoil heap reclaimed." The signature at the bottom is mine. I look at it while the roar in my head rises to a ringing, all the voices at once, and then at four-twelve it breaks off, all of it, at once. For the first time since I was seven my head is quiet.

For half a minute the fog over the backwater grows light, and in the black water I see a sunny noon, sand and the white snag. Then it all goes dark. The floor under my feet is warm, as always.

The signature on the screen grows pale. I don't realize right away that it's the finger pointing at it that's growing pale, and the scar on my palm, and the sleeve of my bathrobe. I take the stylus and trace the signature letter by letter, slowly, the way my father wrote out his surname on a work order. Samokh

! Dead end "The Spoil Heap." You signed that it wasn't burning, and lived your life on what was burning. At four-twelve no one went into the Black Backwater by the dry Tom, and the boy there drowned in '96. My Life shows where else you could have turned.
