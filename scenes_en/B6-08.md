The "Those Who Remember" channel was sold in '25. It was bought by a foundation from Novosibirsk, Siberian Resonance, with polite people in good jackets. They "study the phenomenon." They paid enough for a new roof for the hermitage, a motorboat and a road to Myski. Since then the channel has been writing that I don't die.

::if F_В605 == ВИТЬКА

::end

::if F_В605 != ВИТЬКА
The channel was sold by that girl from Novokuznetsk, a woman with two kids now. She brought me the money in a supermarket bag. I didn't take it. She left the bag on the porch.

I don't die, the channel writes. I've lived thirty years in the taiga without a doctor, I've been in an avalanche and don't remember, I hear the dead, I'm a little bit dead myself. People read it. Twenty thousand, forty, a hundred. For Epiphany they gathered in Myski, on the ice of the Tom, where the Mras-Su flows into it, and invited me. To plunge into the Epiphany hole in front of everyone. Three times, as is proper.

::if F_В607 == ГНАЛ
I once drove them away from the hermitage. They didn't take offense; they decided that's how it should be, that saints drive people away.

There are three hundred of them, maybe more. They stand in a ring around a cross cut into the ice, holding their phones over their heads. Thirty-four below. Steam pours from the hole, and the water in it is black, like in the backwater. A ladder goes down into the blackness.

I'm forty-one. I stand at the edge in just a shirt, barefoot on the ice, and my toes are no longer mine.

"Andrei Nikolayevich," says a man from the foundation, in a good jacket, with a tablet. "Don't rush. Let everyone film."

"Andryukh," he says. "Show them. You don't die. I've seen it."
::end

## После A
The first time I go under the water headfirst and come up. The crowd gasps. The second time, and I come up again. On the third they put their palms on my shoulders and hold me.

I dig in. Under the water I plant my feet on the last rung of the ladder, grab somebody's wrists and push them up, away from me, the way you push up a root-cellar lid. The hands above let go. I come out of the water all in steam and say to them so it carries to the bank:

"Enough."

Three hundred people go quiet. I walk across the ice to the cars barefoot, and nobody holds me back. My feet take a week to recover; the nails on them turn black. The channel writes that I came out of the water dry. I don't read it.

## После B
"I die," I say. My voice gives out in the frost, and I repeat it louder. "I die, like you. A hundred times already. I just remember what it was like. And you'd remember too, if you listened."

At first, silence. Then some woman in a downy shawl starts to cry, loudly, sobbing. Then another. The man from the foundation lowers his tablet.

I don't go into the hole. People leave slowly, by twos and threes, and many come up to me and silently shake my hand. One old woman says, "Son, at least put some socks on." I put them on.

## После C
I raise my hand, and Nika comes across the ice, through the ring of people, elbowing them aside. She comes up to me, takes off her red puffer jacket and puts it on me.

"This is my patient," she says loudly, to everyone, into the foundation man's face. "I'm a doctor. He's had a sleep disorder since he was thirteen. He doesn't die; he doesn't sleep. Disperse, citizens, the session is over."

Someone laughs. Someone swears. The foundation man photographs her, and she gives his camera the middle finger. In her car the heater is running, and it smells of her perfume and coffee from a thermos. She rubs my feet and is silent all the way to the Mras-Su.

## После D
I turn around and walk across the ice, up the Mras-Su, barefoot, then in felt boots some boy silently pushes at me. They catch up with me, call to me; someone shouts that I'm a coward. Vitka doesn't catch up.

## После E
"A rope," I tell the man from the foundation. "Tie it around me. And hold the end yourself. Tight."

He looks surprised, and then understands. I'm worth more to him alive than to all these people. He winds the rope twice around his hand, like reins.

Three times I go under, and on the third they put their palms on my shoulders again. I remember those palms, warm, gentle. The foundation man yanks the rope so hard my ribs crack and pulls me out from under the hands like a cork. He swears at the crowd, at Vitka, at everyone. Clearly he's the one who'd have to answer for me.
