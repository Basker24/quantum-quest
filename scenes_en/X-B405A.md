I pull Vitka out from under the prop and drag him. He's heavy, wet; his leg drags and catches on the coal. I pull him backward toward the shaft, and the rustling isn't in the next room anymore but right above us. The props go past me the other way: sixth, fifth, fourth. The square of sky in the shaft is already visible, white, like a movie screen.

At the third prop from the shaft the roof comes down. It doesn't come down on Vitka; it comes down on me, on my back, my shoulders, softly, heavily and all at once. My knees buckle on their own; my face goes into the coal. Vitka is shouting somewhere under my stomach; I feel him rather than hear him.

I can't breathe; my chest won't rise; the birch grove is lying on it, and the stream, and Shurup's Bychok. Something knocks in my ear, slower and slower. It smells of coal dust, like Mom's hair when she comes home from the processing plant and leans over me before sleep.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm on my knees at the face again, at the twelfth prop, and Vitka is saying, "My leg's pinned. Give it a yank, huh?"

My back hurts as if the seam were still lying on it. I look back down the drift, at the third prop from the shaft, where it crushed me. I didn't make it to the ladder there; I was six meters short.

But here, above my head, is gray sandstone, a slab, and the old props under it have stood for twenty years. The props under it are gray, dry, not one of them cracked. The ones by the shaft were fresh, yellow, and crackled like twigs in a fire.
