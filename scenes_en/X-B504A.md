The water is warm as a bathhouse, and slippery underfoot with silt. I go first, Ploy behind me, holding my belt. Forty steps. On the forty-first the water around me starts to prickle, finely, like soda.

Then something hits me behind the knees. Both legs cramp at once, from the heels to the belly, so they turn to wood. I fall face first into the water and want to get up, but I have nothing to get up with. My arms aren't mine either. They jerk by themselves, fast and small, and slap the water as if I were swimming breaststroke.

Through the water I hear Ploy screaming. She's behind me, far off; the boatman has caught her by the arm and won't let her go. Good that he won't.

My heart is beating wrong, in a fast, fine patter, and then it stops beating altogether. My teeth are ringing, as if I were chewing foil. Under the water someone is whispering, many voices at once, all in Russian, though nobody in this soi speaks Russian. For some reason it smells of Mom, of coal dust.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm sitting in the boat by the bridge again, and Ploy is already swinging a leg over the side.

My legs aren't mine. I pinch my calf, and there's no pain, only a tightness, like before a cramp. The fingers on my hands clench and unclench on their own.

I remember the soda around me on the forty-first step. I remember Ploy screaming and the boatman holding her arm. My calves cramp the moment I look at the water.
