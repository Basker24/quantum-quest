I go up to the top corner, to the junction. The instrument on my belt suddenly starts beeping without a break, on one note. I look at the screen, it says four point two, and I have time to be surprised, because the sensor on the prop is silent. Then I see the bag. Gray with dust, on blue tape, three turns, like a bandage. My three turns.

Down at the bottom of the face, by the shearer, a cutting pick strikes pyrite, like a match against a matchbox.

The fire comes up the face in an even wall, faster than I can turn around. It goes into my mouth and nose at the same time, and my eyelashes crackle. I'm torn off the floor and slammed back against a prop, under the sensor. The last thing I see is the bag. It melts and drips onto my sleeve.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing by the shearer again, and Seryoga is showing me Mishka on his phone, in the bobble hat.

I look up toward the top corner, where I was going to go. You can't see the sensor from here. But I know what's on it, and whose three turns of tape those are.

It hung there for a whole year, and I stopped noticing it after a week. A drop of hot plastic still burns my arm through the sleeve, though the sleeve is whole.

The back of my neck pulls me there, past this minute, to February, to that shift at this same junction. Bondarenko says "just till the end of the shift," and the bag rustles in my fingers.
