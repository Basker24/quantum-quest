The snow ridge on the shoulder is waist-high, so I have to walk on the very edge of the asphalt, in the packed rut. Behind me a horn gives a short beep. I turn around. On the curve a gray Toyota is sliding sideways, slowly, like in a dream, straight at us.

I shove the stroller into the snowbank with both hands, as hard as I can, and it sinks in up to the wheels. I don't have time to jump clear myself. The bumper hits me behind the knees, my legs fold the wrong way, I'm thrown onto the hood and then onto the asphalt, the back of my head first.

The car stops a meter from me. Somebody inside is shouting. I can't turn my head, and I look up at the sky, white as the ceiling in the maternity ward. Right next to me, in the snowbank, Varya has woken up and is screaming, loud and deep, and I lie there and can't answer her.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing at the corner of the building again, holding the stroller handle, and Varya smacks her lips in her sleep.

I remember my legs folding the wrong way. I shift from foot to foot, stamp a felt boot on the ice, and both legs are there, both bending the right way.

I remember the gray Toyota coming at us sideways, and Varya screaming in the snowbank while I lay on my back and couldn't turn my head. Behind the garages the grader is grinding along. Soon the cars will come after it.

The Toyota came around the curve by the bus stop right behind the grader, on the freshly packed snow. The grader grinds on, and my knees twitch by themselves, the way they did when they folded.
