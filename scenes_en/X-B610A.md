I run uphill, toward the bald peak. First along the trail, then off it, over dry moss, over rocks, grabbing at bushes. At sixty-seven my heart pounds in my throat, and I stop every hundred steps. Behind me it crackles, and the crackling is getting closer.

The moss around me starts smoking before I see the fire. Thin wisps, from under my feet, as if the ground had lit a cigarette. Then it flares, all at once, ten meters around, low, blue. I run over fire, and the soles of my boots stick to the rocks.

Three hundred meters to the boulder field. I can see the rocks, gray, bare, cool-looking. The air gets so hot it hurts to breathe, and I stop breathing. A pine to my right goes up from crown to root in a single second, with a roar, like the gas water heater in Mom's kitchen.

I fall face first into burning moss. It smells of singed hair, mine. And behind that, through it, of Mom's hair, coal dust, as if she were standing beside me about to brush off my head.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing by the cabin again, and rust-colored smoke is rising over the valley from below.

My face is burning, especially my left cheek, the one I fell on. I touch it. Skin like any skin, stubble, old man's spots. But my fingers remember how hot it was under them.

My calves are rock-hard, as after a long climb. The heat was always below, at my heels, and with every step up it came closer. The moss smoked under my feet before I saw a flame, so by then the fire was already everywhere, under me and around me.
