The water is ice-cold, and the cold squeezes my chest at once like a hoop. I swim along the wall, holding a cable on hooks with one hand. The flashlight reaches half a meter and stops in the murk, and something white floats in the murk, in flakes. At fifteen meters the cable ends, torn off, and my hand grabs nothing.

I swim on without the cable, by a memory of the plan that I don't have. The corridor turns, I hit my shoulder on the corner and lose which way is up. The rope at my waist snags on something and won't let me back. I yank it and yank it, and the air goes out of me in bubbles, ticklish, through my nose.

I don't take the next breath; my body takes it for me. Water comes into my throat, cold and heavy, tasting of rust. Everything like when I was seven. Black water, crowded, and right by my ear somebody starts counting. Only the girl who counted isn't here.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing at the edge of the corridor again, knee-deep in black water, and Gosha is asking whether to put in the pump.

The water is inside me. I cough and cough and bring nothing up; my lungs are clear. Rustam pounds me on the back and asks if I've swallowed some dust.

The cable on its hooks ends at fifteen meters. At twenty my helmet knocked against something under the ceiling, hollow, with emptiness inside it, and I swam on, down, after the cable. The top of my head still remembers that sound.
