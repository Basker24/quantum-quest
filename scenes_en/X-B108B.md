Seryoga and I are pulling the cable; he's cursing the shearer, and the shearer is cursing the seam. A cutting pick scrapes something hard and strikes a spark, small, like from a lighter, and the whole face flares up, from the shearer to the top corner.

I don't hear the explosion. The blast simply lays me flat on the conveyor, and above, where the roof was, there's suddenly nothing. The support sections fold one after another, like dominoes, with iron clangs, and my turn comes.

The roof doesn't settle on my back all at once but gradually, as if somebody heavy were getting comfortable. Seryoga is somewhere close, under the same roof, calling me by my last name. I want to answer. There's no air left for an answer, and it smells of Mom's hair.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing by the shearer again. Seryoga takes off his glove and shows me Mishka on his phone.

He's alive, both his arms are there, the left one holding the phone, the right one poking at the screen. I look at his left arm longer than I should, and he asks what's up with me.

The spark came from the pick. It'll come from the pick if the shearer doesn't stop. And it'll only stop if the sensor sees the gas. I know why it doesn't.

Plastic rustles in my fingers, a Maria-Ra grocery bag that isn't here. February '15, the junction, Bondarenko. I don't understand why I'm remembering it now, but the back of my neck aches like a sore tooth and pulls me there.
