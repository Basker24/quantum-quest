Graduation night ends on the river embankment, same as everybody's. Girls in dresses from the Assorti store, guys in jackets they'll grow into, Sovetskoye champagne straight from the bottle, smuggled past our homeroom teacher up somebody's sleeve. Our homeroom teacher pretends not to see and keeps digging in her purse for a handkerchief.

Galina Ivanovna, our astronomy teacher, walks among us in a shawl, handing out parting words. To me she says the same thing she told the whole class in our last lesson:

"Halley's Comet comes back on the twenty-eighth of July, twenty sixty-one. You'll be seventy-two. Live that long, and you'll see it."

Everybody cracks up. Seventy-two is never.

::if F_Т09 == ДОЛГ_СВАРНОМУ
Vitka's here too, though he graduated a year ahead of us. He came "to see us off," in Welder's leather jacket, and he's handing out Parliaments to everybody. Now the two of us owe Welder together, and it hangs over us like the smog over the Kirovsky district.
::end

::if F_Т09 != ДОЛГ_СВАРНОМУ
Vitka is doing his service near Chita. A month ago he sent a half-page letter, and half of it was blacked out by the military censor, or maybe by Vitka himself.
::end

The sky over the Tom goes pink. And then everybody's Nokias beep at once, some alarms, some "congrats" texts. My 3310 says 4:00. I blink, and it says 4:12. I blink again, and it still says 4:12.

::if F_Т09 == НИКА
A text from Nika, in Novosibirsk: "Awake? Me too. Looking at the river. Ours is different." I don't answer for ten minutes, because I don't know how.
::end

On the far bank somebody's standing with a fishing rod, or maybe it's a bush. While I'm trying to make it out, mist comes up off the river, and the far bank disappears.

> Eighteen is the right age to decide who you're going to be, so I decided, differently in every world. In one of them I probably got it right. If only I knew which.
