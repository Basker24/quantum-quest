The Nagant is heavy and warm from someone else's hand. I spin the cylinder; it clicks like a bicycle wheel when you turn the bike upside down on its seat and spin it. The metal at my temple is colder than I thought. The onlookers aren't breathing. Across from me Tyoma closes his eyes and moves his lips.

I don't hear the click.

I see the felt very close up, green, pilled, with a cookie crumb in it. Someone above me shouts "that's it, that's it," and chairs scrape on the concrete. Tyoma is holding my hand across the table, tight, and crying. It smells of gunpowder, and under it of coal dust, Mom's hair. The felt darkens from the edge.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm sitting at the green table again, and Baldy is laying the Nagant on the felt, grip toward me. "Guest goes first."

I remember the cookie crumb in the felt. I remember how Tyoma held my hand. My temple is ice-cold, and I rub it with my palm until it burns.

The one who sat down at this table first stayed lying on the felt. That was me, and now it isn't me. If I spin again, the one who gets up from the table will be me, and someone else will have to lie down. I know that as surely as I know there's a staircase up behind me.
