I never did leave the island. The relief crews changed, young ones, from Tiksi, from Yakutsk, from Novosibirsk, but I stayed, and after a few years they stopped asking when I was taking leave. Twice they wanted to close the station, first because of money, then because of the Wager, the war the radio calls that because in it they bet on people who don't die. Both times they forgot. People forget about me easily here, and I like that.

On the twenty-seventh of July my relief flew to the mainland, to his family, to watch the comet with his kids. I stayed alone. The night here now is white; the sun barely touches the sea in the north and rises again. And in the south, low over the water, hangs the comet, bright, with a tail, and it seems to me the tail points south, to where, two thousand kilometers away, my Tom flows, or no longer flows.

I'm drawn where the tail points. It has drawn me all this last year, the way the field used to draw me in April, when there was still snow in the city. I sit on the station steps with the hammer on my knees. The very one Zoya Arkadyevna gave me on the porch in Mundybash. Fifty-four years I've had it.

At five to four I get up and tap the stone by the steps with it. The stone rings. The ringing in my head doesn't end, and in it are all of mine, at once, every one, and all the strangers, and all of them silent. They don't call. They wait, the way you wait at a level crossing when the barrier is already down and the train isn't in sight yet.

At four-twelve the ringing breaks off all at once, the way a line goes slack when a big fish has slipped off the other end.

For half a minute the sea in front of me grows light. Under the ice floes I see sand, yellow, sunlit, and a white snag like a hand, which can't possibly be in the Laptev Sea. Then it all goes dark. For the first time since I was seven my head is completely quiet, quieter than this island ever was on the deepest winter night.

I open the watch log to the last page. In the "Time" column I write "04:12." In the "Phenomena" column I write "Silence." The pen in my fingers grows light, and then I stop feeling it. The letters on the page grow pale, and I don't realize right away that it's the fingers that wrote them growing pale, and the scar on my palm, and the hammer on the step. The comet is clearly visible. Silen
