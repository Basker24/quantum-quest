I lie flat on the coal, and the belt carries me into the dark. The arches flash so low overhead that I press my helmet into the coal. Before the crosscut the belt tilts up toward the transfer point, and I get up on all fours to jump sideways.

My knees slide apart on the fine coal. I push off, but there's nothing to push off from; the coal moves with me. A rubber boot catches on the edge of the belt, and I'm swung around feet first. I see the drum whole, in the light of my lamp, wet, black, turning evenly and not fast.

My boot goes under it first, with a crunch, like a dry board broken over a knee. I grab the frame with my hands, and my fingernails tear off one after another. At first there's no pain, just a pull downward, steady and smooth, like a winch. Then the pain comes all at once, and I scream. My lamp hits the roof, and the roof spins.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing at the face by the shearer again, the tablet beeping, and Vitka saying through his mouthpiece, "Boss. Well?"

My right leg is burning up to the hip, though it's whole. I shift from foot to foot and can't stop.

There's nothing to hold on to on the coal; my palms know that now. And along the frame before the transfer point, at shoulder height, ran a red emergency-stop cord. I flew right past it and didn't even turn my head.
