I've worked as an orderly since January '13. I mop floors, push gurneys, hold the hands of those whose hands need holding. At night I'm on duty in the men's ward, on the second floor, in the very room where I lay myself. The crack in the ceiling shaped like Italy is still there; nobody ever plastered it over.

I take the pills every evening, in front of the nurse. Behind them the voices stand quietly, like people behind a door who stopped knocking long ago. Sometimes, at four in the morning, I hear that they're there. You can't make out the words. It's enough for me that they're there, and that you can't make out the words.

::if F_В601 == ТЕТРАДЬ
I gave the green notebook back to her when I came to work, and it lies in Anna Markovna's drawer. Once a year, on my birthday, she takes it out, and together we look at the three columns. No new lines have been added under "Who" since 2012.
::end

Anna Markovna retires in '27. As she leaves she gives me her glass holder and says, "You were my best orderly, Andrei. And my most difficult patient." She dies six years later. At the funeral I stand right by the fence, and afterward her daughter says her mother used to talk about me and that I "coped."

Dad dies in '26, on the balcony, at four-twelve, with an unlit cigarette. Mom in '50, in her sleep. Sonka visits me on Sundays, with her grandchildren, and the grandchildren call me "Grandpa Orderly." I stop working in '51, and they give me a room at the hospital, and later a bed in the ward for those with nowhere else to go. It's the same room. I don't mind.

In '47 the TV in the day room starts showing the Orpheus ad: a calm face, a white coat, "you don't have to die." The patients watch it like a soap opera. Then, in '55, the same TV shows the Wager, and the patients watch that too, the same way. I don't care. I don't hear those voices. Somewhere, maybe, strangers who don't die are speaking in my pitch. The pills hold them back too.

In the winter of '58 I'm lying by the window. Heart, the nurse on duty said, and nothing to be done about it, it's age. Outside is the old hospital garden, apple trees, snow falling slowly, in big flakes, like in a cartoon. I watch it settle on the branches. The branches bend and hold.

Toward morning, at four-twelve, someone knocks on the door in my head for the last time. Quietly, politely, once. I don't open it. That's my right, and Anna Markovna would have agreed with me.

The snow keeps falling. Three years until the comet.

! Dead end "Silence." This version of you never reached the Black Backwater, and at four-twelve on the twenty-eighth of July 2061 nobody will dive in there. My Life shows where else you could have turned.
