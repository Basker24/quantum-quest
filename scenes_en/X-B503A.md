The scooter picks up speed on the slope by itself. I squeeze the rear brake, and the lever sinks all the way to the handlebar, soft as modeling clay. I squeeze the front, and the wheel skids on the sand. The left-hand bend leaps out of the dark, and I'm carried to the right, into the middle, where my lane is back home in Kemerovo.

Headlights hit from below. I have time to see a radiator grille, chrome, with the word on it, Toyota, and sacks of ice in the back.

The impact takes me in the chest and face at once. The handlebars break in my hands; I hear it, like a dry stick broken over a knee. Then I'm lying on the concrete, cheek in the sand. Ice from a burst sack has spilled all around; the cubes are melting on the warm road, and one lies right in front of my eye.

The old driver kneels over me saying something in Thai, very fast. I want to tell him it's all right, it was my own fault. Something warm comes out of my mouth. The ice cube by my eye is melting, and the headlights are reflected in it. Someone nearby is whispering, many someones, and the last thing is the smell of Mom, her coal-dust hair.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm sitting on the scooter by the bar again, the key already in the ignition, the engine purring.

I switch it off. My hands go to my face on their own, feeling my nose, cheekbones, teeth. Everything's there. There's no blood in my mouth, only rum.

I remember the chrome grille and the ice cube right by my eye, with the headlights in it. I remember the handlebars breaking. In about ten minutes that pickup will be at that bend. It's there every night.
