I throw down the box and run. The ice underfoot is black, packed by tires; I run on it like on glass, arms flailing. The chain-link fence is high, with barbed wire along the top. I jump onto it, grab hold, my boots slipping in the mesh.

The dog runs along the fence without a sound. The ring on the cable shrieks along behind her. She jumps and catches my boot, by the heel, and hangs there. I jerk my leg, lose the mesh with one hand, then the other.

I fall backward from twice my height, and the back of my head comes down on the corner of the dock, on its iron edging. I hear the sound from inside my head. Then there's a flashlight above me, and the man in the sheepskin says, "Oh, for the love of…" It smells of Mom's hair, of coal dust, and the dog is licking my hand.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing in the doorway of the cold room again with the forty-second box in my arms, and the flashlight is crawling across the yard.

The back of my head hurts as if I were standing with it against a stove. I touch it with my glove; there's nothing there, just my hat and hair. And the dog is running along the fence, and now I look past the dog, at the cable. The cable ends at the lamppost, five meters from the corner of the warehouse. The ring can't go past the post.
