I take him on. The first month he hauls crates and asks no questions. The second I sit him beside me at the table, under the umbrella, and he listens to how I talk to people. The third he tells some guy himself not to buy the garage by the station, and the guy listens.

I teach him everything I know. Who owes whom, and how much. Who'll rise in 2010 and who'll go to prison in '12. Where the market is weak and where it's strong, whom to hit right away and whom later. I don't tell him how I know. He thinks the old man is just smart, and tries to become the same.

::if F_П102 == КРОВЬ
I give him the thermos from the freezer on his twenty-fifth birthday. "This is yours," I say. "You'll understand later." He doesn't understand, and puts it in his own freezer, behind the pelmeni.
::end

He learns fast, faster than I did. By thirty he runs the market on Shakhtyorov and half the garages. People no longer call him Andryukha but by his patronymic. His mother hasn't spoken to him since he gave her a car and she asked whose money paid for it.

I die in 2012, in March, in my apartment, at four-twelve in the morning, with no fork. He buries me like a father, at the Kirov cemetery, with wreaths from the whole market. The headstone carries the surname from my passport. Someone else's.

What happens next I know without the Choir, because I once stood there myself. In 2061 a gray, heavyset man with a gold signet ring will come to the dry riverbed in a big car, send his driver away and go down to the backwater alone. He'll stand by the water looking at the snag, and at four-twelve he'll dive. Under the night surface there'll be noon, and the boy by the snag, and the girl counting. He'll pull the boy out, and then he'll go into the willows. There he'll stop and look at 1996 as at a market stall.

The wolf raised a wolf. And however much you feed a wolf, it still looks to the forest.

! Ending "The Boss." A wolf's life that raised itself an heir. My Life shows what other roads led to this morning.
