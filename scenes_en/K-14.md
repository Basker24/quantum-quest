I part the branches and say through the bushes, quietly, so only he can hear:

"Don't be afraid of water, Andryusha. You won't drown in it."

The boy turns his head toward me. The girl does too. I let go of the branches, and they close.

The seam closes before I can take a step. I come out into 2061, onto the dry riverbed, and at first it seems to me that everything is in place: the fog, the stones, the backwater, the comet. There's no one on the bank. No thermos, no blanket, no footprints.

I walk home to Rudnichny, and the street is the same, and the building entrance is the same. The door code is different. In the list of residents by the intercom, in our apartment, there's a stranger's surname. I stand by the door until someone comes out with a dog and holds the door for me, taking me for a neighbor.

The boy I told he wouldn't drown grew up unafraid of water. In the life he lived, he went into it one time too many, at about twelve, on the Tom, and didn't come up. The seam doesn't forgive anyone a word too many. The loop I was supposed to close exactly as it opened has now opened in another world, and this one is left without me.

I sit down on the bench by the entrance, where the old woman from the ground floor used to sit, and sit there until the sun comes up.

! Dead end "One Word Too Many." The loop closes only if you repeat in the water exactly what you saw in '96. One word too many, and your home stayed in another world.
