The first time I go under the water headfirst and come up. The water is so cold it hits my chest like a plank. The crowd gasps. The second time, and I come up again, and someone on the bank starts to sing.

On the third time palms settle on my shoulders. Warm, many at once, and they hold me gently, the way you hold a child while washing its hair. From above, through the water, I hear them counting, in chorus, three hundred throats: "Twenty-one. Twenty-two." They know about the two hundred and fifty-two. I told them myself, long ago.

::if F_В605 == ВИТЬКА
One pair of palms I recognize. Big, with hard calluses from the steering wheel, with a wedding ring Vitka still wears though he's divorced. He holds on tighter than anyone. He believes.
::end

I brace against the ladder and push up, and they hold me. Not out of malice; they just want to finish counting. The air comes out of me in bubbles, and they rise and burst under their fingers. At a hundred I open my mouth.

The water comes into my chest, heavy, icy, and everything in my chest clenches, as if cinched with a belt. Up above they're counting: "A hundred and forty. A hundred and forty-one." It smells of Mom's hair, coal dust, through the water, through the ice, through thirty-five years. They finished counting without me.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing at the edge of the ice hole again in just a shirt, and the man from the foundation is saying, "Don't rush. Let everyone film."

My shoulders burn from other people's palms. I look at them, at these three hundred people with phones, and I see their hands. All the hands at once. Hands in mittens, hands without mittens, hands raising their phones right now.

They held me out of love. They wanted to count to two hundred and fifty-two, so it would all be true, like back then. I told them that number myself, by a campfire, long ago, and now I can't take it back.
