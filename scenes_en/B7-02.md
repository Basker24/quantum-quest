By the spring of 2010 I'm a grade-four operator, no more "trainee," and in night school I'm moving up to third year. Buttoning my jacket for me in the front hall, the way he did in first grade, Dad says, "Get married, Andryukha. Or you'll drink yourself to death on that ammonia of yours, like everybody." He drinks on Fridays himself, moderately and on schedule, like down the mine.

::if F_Т09 == ДОЛГ_СВАРНОМУ
Vitka and I had worked off our debt to Welder by the spring of '09. Welder shook our hands with that soft warm palm of his and said, "You're free, boys." What was in those crates I never found out, and I don't plan to.
::end

::if F_В701 == НИКА
I've got a whole winter-boot box full of Nika's letters, about forty of them. Two New Year's she spent at Vitka's mom's, and both times I saw her off at the station and both times didn't know what to say on the platform. In March she transferred from Novosibirsk to our medical academy, fourth year. "Not because of you, Samokhin, don't flatter yourself. The dorms are better here." In April, in my parents' kitchen, she said in front of Mom, "Are you going to propose, or do I have to do it myself?" Mom dropped the ladle.
::end

::if F_В701 == ОЛЯ
Olya and I have been going out for two and a half years, since that *Saw*. She knows my shift schedule better than I do, and after a night shift she meets me at the bus stop with meat pies from Maria-Ra, still warm. Mom loves her for being "a good homemaker," Dad because she laughs at his jokes, even the old ones. And Sonka secretly wears her lipstick.
::end

::if F_В701 == СМЕНА
Olya from the lab and I have been seeing each other on a schedule for three years now. Every other time her shifts line up with mine, and then she comes into the operator room with her sampler and stands by my panel a minute longer than a sample takes. Gena pretends not to see, and at New Year's he announces in front of the whole unit, "Marry her while I'm in a good mood. She's my only niece."
::end

We set the wedding for July. The union committee at Azot gives us the plant canteen at half price, Mom spends a week chopping Olivier salad by the basinful and hides the vodka from Dad in the washing machine. We buy a suit at the Central Market, black, with sequins on the lapels. The seller swears you can't see the sequins, and you can see them from the other end of the row.

::if СВЯЗЬ_ВИТЬКА >= 30
Vitka is going to be my best man. He's bought himself a pink tie and wears it even to work, to get used to it.
::end

::if СВЯЗЬ_ВИТЬКА < 30
Over the years Vitka and I drifted apart, I don't know how, without any falling-out. My best man is going to be Gena Shubin, in his one and only suit, the one he got married in himself.
::end

> In the end it was Dad who tied my tie, in the kitchen, without a word, in one motion. I don't remember any other advice he gave me about married life.

## После A
We sign the register at the Wedding Palace. Nika is in a dress her mother carried on her lap from Novosibirsk, all four hours on the bus. The canteen smells of bleach and herring salad, there are balloons on the tile wall. The guests shout "Gorko!" (it means "bitter," and you have to kiss to sweeten it) and count out loud together. At thirty Nika pulls away from me and whispers, "I've counted higher than that, Samokhin."

::if СВЯЗЬ_ВИТЬКА >= 30
Vitka in his pink tie gives a toast, loses his place, waves his hand and just says, "You're both nuts. Live a long time." He kisses his cousin on the forehead and slaps me on the back so hard I choke on my champagne.
::end

::if lived(В1-09)
Toward morning Vitka finds me on the steps, sits down next to me and rolls a cigarette between his fingers for a long time. "Listen. I keep dreaming about the mine, like a beam's got me pinned, it's dark, and you're sitting there next to me. What's that supposed to mean, huh?" He lights up and doesn't say any more about it.
::end

::if СВЯЗЬ_ВИТЬКА < 30
Vitka sits on the bride's side, as her cousin, and hardly talks to me all evening. Only at the end, already in his jacket, he shakes my hand and says, "Take care of her, Vodyanoy." Gena Shubin dances with my mom to "Akh, eta svadba," the song every Russian wedding plays, and steps on her feet.
::end

Sonka catches the bouquet. She's twelve and so embarrassed she nearly cries. Toward morning Dad sits on the canteen steps, smoking and looking at the Azot stacks, where the lights burn all night.

## После B
We sign the register at the Wedding Palace. Olya rented her dress from a bridal shop on Kirov Street, her veil keeps slipping, and once it falls right into the salad. Olya laughs so hard the whole canteen laughs with her, even the waitresses. The guests shout "Gorko!" (it means "bitter," and you have to kiss to sweeten it) and count, and at twenty Olya says, "That's it, my mascara."

::if СВЯЗЬ_ВИТЬКА >= 30
Vitka in his pink tie gives a toast, loses his place, waves his hand and just says, "Andryukha, you're a lucky bastard. Don't forget it." Then he spends half the evening teaching Olya's dad to drink with his arm hooked through somebody else's.
::end
