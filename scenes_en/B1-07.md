In '15 the ruble falls by half, coal gets cheaper abroad, and the holding company announces that bonuses will only be paid to sections that make quota. Longwall seven doesn't make quota. It's gassy. Methane comes out of the seam steadily and stubbornly; the fixed sensor at the junction, where the face meets the ventilation drift, reads one and a half percent and cuts the power to the shearer. Twenty minutes later the gas thins out and the shearer goes back on. Forty minutes after that it all happens again. Over a shift that's three hours of downtime.

On Saturday Sonka comes to see me, without calling, with a backpack, in Mom's jacket. She's seventeen, graduating in the spring. She sits on the windowsill, finishing my dumplings straight from the frying pan, cold, and says she's going to medical school, in Novosibirsk, and hasn't told Mom yet. "Just don't be like Dad," she says, already at the door. "He still smokes on the balcony at night. I hear him."

The section foreman, Bondarenko, fat and honest in his fatness, catches me at the junction.

::if F_В105 == ВГСЧ
I'm here with the unit for an inspection, measuring gas along the whole face with my own instrument and writing it up. There's a plastic bag over the sensor at the junction, tied on with blue electrical tape. I take it off to check the sensor and see that it works fine. Bondarenko comes over while I'm holding the bag.
::end

::if F_В105 == БРИГАДА
I'm here on Dad's team; Dad's off today. Bondarenko takes a plastic bag and a roll of blue electrical tape out of his pocket and holds them out to me, because I'm standing closest to the sensor.
::end

"Samokhin," he says. "Just till the end of the shift. Put it on and tape it up. The gas won't go over two, I promise you, I'll stand here with the instrument myself. The guys have mortgages. Pakhomov's got a kid on the way. Come on, Samokhin. You're one of us."

::if F_Т08 == ДАТЧИКИ
I've heard this conversation before, in '05, in the smoking room by the pithead building. That time a foreman said under his breath that the sensors in the section had been taped over to make quota, and out of twelve men they brought nine up alive. I told it all to the reporter back then. They still remember the name Samokhin at this mine.
::end

Seryoga, at the shearer, pretends he isn't listening. The bag rustles in my fingers. The face is silent, waiting to be allowed to work again.

> The bag was from Maria-Ra, with handles. For about ten years after, I saw bags like that in every dream I had about the mine.

## После A
I put the bag over the sensor and wind the tape around it, three turns, like a bandage. My fingers feel like cotton, and the tape sticks to my glove. Bondarenko slaps me on the shoulder: "Good man." The shearer comes on and doesn't stop once for the rest of the shift.

::if F_В105 == ВГСЧ
In the inspection report I write "no comments" and sign. The pen writes badly, and I go over my signature twice.
::end

That night I don't sleep; I listen to Dad going out on the balcony to smoke. At four-twelve I go out too. He holds out the pack to me without asking, and we smoke together in silence. I can't bring myself to tell him about the bag.

## После B
"No," I say, and put the bag in Bondarenko's breast pocket. "Let it stand."

::if F_В105 == ВГСЧ
In the report I write the sensor, the bag, the tape and the time, to the minute. The face is stopped for three days for inspection. Bondarenko gets a reprimand, the section loses its bonus, and for two months the men from seven don't say hello to me in the bathhouse.
::end

::if F_В105 == БРИГАДА
Bondarenko puts me on nights for a week, on cleanup, where they put the guys being punished. For two months the men from seven don't say hello to me in the bathhouse. Seryoga does, but he looks away.
::end

::if F_Т08 == ДАТЧИКИ
In March the director calls me in, a new one, young, in rimless glasses. He looks at his papers for a long time and then says, "Samokhin. Was it you who talked to the TV people in '05?" I say it was. He nods and lets me go. Nothing happens, and that makes it worse.
::end

## После C
"All right," I say, and wind the tape, three turns.

That night I come to the lamp room without a work order. Tanya is behind the window. She looks at me, then at the clock, then hands me a lamp and a self-rescuer without asking anything, and writes something illegible in the log. "Bring it back," she says. She doesn't say "good boy" anymore.

I go down with the night shift, get to seven, rip the bag off the sensor and carry it out in my pocket. In the morning Bondarenko yells all over the section about "that bastard from the night shift." The face gets gassy again and stops. Nobody in the section ever finds out who it was. Tanya knows. Later I throw the bag in the trash can outside her building.
