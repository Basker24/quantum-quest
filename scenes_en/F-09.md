"Go to your Tomsk," I say. "And don't let me see you at this market again. Here they'll make me out of you."

He doesn't understand, takes offense, goes red to the neck and leaves without saying goodbye. A week later I'm told he got on the commuter train to Tomsk. I pay for his dorm through an acquaintance in the dean's office. Let him think he just got lucky.
::end

::if F_П103 == ПРАВДА
I tell him everything, from the snag. About the backwater, the two hundred and fifty-two, the old man in the striped shirt who walked out of the willows. That the old man is sitting in front of him. About the test tubes that are gone now, about Granddad, about the Choir. I talk for an hour; he doesn't interrupt.

"You're sick, Grandpa," he says at the end, very politely. "You need treatment."

I agree. He gets up and leaves, but at the corner of the kiosk he looks back and looks at me for a long time. He rubs the scar on his right palm with his left hand, without noticing.
::end

I give the market to whoever grabs it first; I don't need it anymore. I live in a one-room flat on Krasnaya Gorka, by a window that looks out on the Tom. Every summer I walk to the backwater and sit on the stone. Nobody swims in the backwater; everyone remembers how in '96 a kid almost drowned there.

Sometimes they show him to me on local TV: a young scientist, some competition, some prize. He says clever things into the camera and keeps his hands behind his back.

I die in 2013, in February, at a bus stop, in line for the bus. I have no documents on me; I burned my passport when I gave up the market. They bury me as unidentified, under a number, at the edge of the Kirov cemetery.

::if F_П103 == ПРАВДА
In spring there's a carnation lying on the number, one, red, plastic, the kind they sell at the gates for ten rubles. The cemetery watchman sees a young man with a scar on his palm lay it there, and then stand by the marker for a long time without taking off his hat, moving his lips as if counting.
::end

::if F_П103 != ПРАВДА
No one ever found out who I was. Maybe that's how it should be.
::end
