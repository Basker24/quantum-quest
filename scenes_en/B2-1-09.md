The Tom in Tomsk runs out by June now. Under the Kommunalny Bridge kids ride bikes along the dry bottom, and under Lagerny Garden, where in '09 I almost stayed on a bench at forty-two below, people graze goats. From Kemerovo they write that the backwater across from Rudnichny is still standing, as if the river forgot to take only that one.

For two weeks now I've been drawn downstream, to where there's no stream anymore. It pulls steady and strong, like a cable being reeled in by a winch down at the bottom of a shaft. The Choir hums without a break, low, like the main ventilation fan at the Glubokaya, and through it, in my pitch, all their Second Chances hum at once.

In the evenings a comet hangs over the dry riverbed. At graduation Galina Ivanovna told us when it would come back, and we cracked up: seventy-two was never.

Yesterday, for the first time, I took the storm jacket out of Dad's parcel. For forty years it lay in the postal box. The canvas is heavy and smells of Grandma's trunk and mothballs. There's a hole in the elbow. I hang it on a nail by the door and look at it all evening.

All his life Lunyov was solving one equation. It had one unknown: the old man in the wet striped shirt who in '96 pulled me out of the water, gave me mouth-to-mouth and walked off into the willows. I wanted to find him all my life too. He will be there, Lunyov wrote. I reread it and only now notice that Lunyov never said who "he" was.

The last bus to Kemerovo leaves at eight. Three hours along the highway beside the dry river, and then half an hour down on foot.

On the bus I sit by the window with the backpack on my knees. Near Yurga, in the dark beyond the glass, the turn-off for the R-255 floats by. For a second the Choir goes quiet on it, all at once, like a class when the principal walks in.

## После B
"Let's go," I tell Nika.

She puts down her crossword, pours boiling water into a thermos and puts on her jacket, all silently and quickly, as if she'd been waiting since morning. She takes the storm jacket off its nail herself and hands it to me.

On the bus she sleeps on my shoulder. Her hair smells of my laundry soap. I don't sleep; I count the kilometer posts until I lose track.

## После C
I leave the key, the notebook and a note for the landlady on the table: "Gone fishing." I take only the storm jacket.

On the bus I sit by the window. The comet hangs over the dry river and doesn't fall behind, like a dog that's followed you home from the market.
