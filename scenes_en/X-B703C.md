Through the garages it's three minutes shorter. A path is trampled between the lockups, steam stands over the heating main, and the snow there is gray and soft. I push the stroller along the trampled part, step onto the smooth white patch, and the smooth patch drops out from under my feet.

I let go of the handle right away. At least I get that right. I fall down into the warm steam, hit my temple on a pipe, and my neck cracks, loud, like a dry branch under a felt boot.

After that I lie on the pipes, in the warmth, and feel nothing below my chin. Up above, in the round hole, there's gray sky and the edge of a wheel. Varya is crying. I listen to her for as long as I can hear.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing at the corner of the building again, holding the stroller handle, and Varya smacks her lips in her sleep.

I remember the crack in my neck. I turn my head right, left, and my neck turns obediently, and my scarf itches the way it always does.

I remember the round hole with gray sky in it, the edge of the wheel, and Varya crying up there while I lay in the warm steam and couldn't even blink back at her. Steam is rising over the garages. Now I know there's no cover on the manhole there, only a crust of snow.

The smooth white patch by the heating main, the one nobody had stepped on: my heel remembers it giving way like the crust on a snowdrift. Right next to it ran somebody else's path, trampled and crooked, and I went around it because my way was shorter.
