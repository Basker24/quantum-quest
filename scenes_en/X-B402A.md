I duck into the cold room and pull the door after me. It moves easily; the spring pulls it, and it slams shut without any more help from me. The lock clicks, echoing, like in a stairwell. The flashlight passes by; I see its yellow strip under the door, then the strip goes out.

On the inside the door has no handle. There's a hole with a rusty pin where a handle used to be. I yank the pin, ram the door with my shoulder, yell. The compressor hums louder than I do.

An hour later I get warm. That's strange, I remember that it's strange, and I don't care. I sit down on the boxes. With my finger in the frost on the wall I write "Mom" and get as far as "Mo." The last thing I see is the chick in the cowboy hat on the box by my face. He's smiling. It smells of coal dust, of Mom's hair when she comes home from a shift.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing in the doorway of the cold room again with the forty-second box in my arms, and the flashlight is crawling across the yard.

I remember how the lock clicked. I remember the pin instead of a handle, rusty; it left rust-colored dust on my palm. I remember how it got warm, and how I liked it. That's why my teeth are chattering now, harder than from the cold.

I look at the door. The spring at the top is thick and rusty, and the bottom of the door is lined with felt so it seals tight.
