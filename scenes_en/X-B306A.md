I walk toward the barrel, toward Gena, talking as I go, loudly, cheerfully, the way you talk to drunks outside your building: guys, it's all fine, we're not an inspection, we dig permafrost, here's the tape, here's the notebook. I reach into my storm jacket pocket for the notebook, to show them.

Slavik sees me reach into my pocket. He gets up with the shotgun, fast, drops his mug. Gena yells something at him, "what are you doing" or "stop," I can't make it out. The shot is very loud, louder than the storm on the golets, and I sit down in the mud, because suddenly my legs won't hold me.

At first there's no pain; it's just hot under my ribs, on the right, as if someone had put a baked potato there. Then I see that the mud under me is turning red. Sardaana screams in Yakut, terribly, and runs to me, and drops to her knees beside me, and presses her tape measure to my side, all of it, in a wad, like a bandage. Above us hangs the mammoth's leg in reddish wool; it's thirty thousand years old, and it doesn't care. It smells of frozen rot, of the river and of coal dust from Mom's hair.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing by the tunnel again, and Slavik by the barrel says for the third time, "Inspection, Uncle Gen."

Under my ribs on the right it's hot. I touch the spot with my hand, and the storm jacket there is dry. I remember how he stood up when I reached into my pocket. I remember Sardaana's tape in my side, with little flowers, red.

Words don't work here when there's a gun in someone's hands and vodka in his mug, and mine are the worst of all if I'm reaching into a pocket while I say them.
