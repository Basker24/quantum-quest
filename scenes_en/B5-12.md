The voice message from Sonka comes at six in the morning, while I'm shaving. Forty-one seconds. I listen to it with shaving foam on my cheek.

"Andryush, hi." Sonka's voice has become Mom's, low and a little hoarse. "Don't call back; our connection's acting up again. It's about Mom. Her memory's been going for two years now, the doctors say dementia, you know. Now it's gone completely. Every day she asks where Andryusha is. I say, in Thailand. And she laughs and says, what Thailand, he's seven, he's gone down to the river with Vitka. And she asks me to go get you before you drown. Every day, Andryush. I go. I go out into the yard and stand there. Okay. That's all. Kisses."

There have been no flights to Russia for three years. In the Andaman Sea, right off our beach, gray ships without flags stand at anchor, and at night their lights burn. The radio calls it the Wager. The war was started by people who bought themselves a "second chance" and are sure they won't die, and now they wager other people's lives, since they aren't afraid for their own. Only military transports fly through Phuket, and there are no tourists, like in 2020.

## После A
I record the voice message four times. The first time my voice gives out, the second I say too much, the third I cough. The fourth comes out at eleven seconds.

"Sonya. I'll come. As soon as the planes are allowed. Tell Mom I didn't drown. Tell her I'm on my way back from the river."

Sonka answers two days later with one word: "Told her." Then, a minute later, a second message: "She said tell him to hurry up or his supper will get cold."

::if F_ПАРТНЁР == НЕТ
I call Ploy on Sunday and tell her about Mom. She listens, says "I'm sorry, Andy," and is silent for a long time. Then she says she'll stay at her sister's for good. I don't argue. We both knew it long ago; nobody had said it out loud.
::end

## После B
I record a video for Mom on the boat. The sea, white sand, fish right at the surface. I dive to five meters with the phone in my hand and show her a turtle. Back up, I say into the camera, "Mom, it's me. Andryusha. I'm swimming, see? I didn't drown."

A week later Sonka writes that Mom watches the video every evening before bed, the way she used to watch Santa Barbara. About the turtle she tells Sonka you won't catch one like that in the Tom. And she doesn't recognize me in the video. She says, "Some nice old man. Swimming."

::if F_ПАРТНЁР == НЕТ
I send the video to Ploy too. She watches it and writes: "You swim good. Still." And then, an hour later: "I stay here. Sorry." I look at those words until it gets dark.
::end

## После D
I take the bus to Chumphon, twelve hours along the sea, past empty hotels. Ploy's sister opens the door to me and goes off to the market, leaving the two of us in the kitchen. I tell Ploy about Mom, about the river, about how for her I'm still seven. I talk for a long time, in Thai, badly.

We ride home together on her scooter, and I sit behind like a passenger, in her helmet. I answer Sonka from a gas station: "I'll come. The two of us."
