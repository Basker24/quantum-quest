I take a step into the willows, toward them, and they part, and then close around me. At first I can still feel my legs, the sand, the wet striped shirt. Then only voices, and among them one new one, mine.

Inside the Choir there's no "then" and no "later." There are all the minutes at once, all the last minutes the Andrei Samokhins had in all the worlds. I hear them just as I heard them from outside all my life, only now I'm among them myself and can answer.

I find the boy. He's seven, standing on the flat stone by the Black Backwater, watching something glint under the water by the snag. I breathe cold onto the back of his neck, and he shivers.

He's eighteen, standing in a stairwell with Vitka, and Vitka has something in his hand that's better not thought about. I breathe onto the back of his neck.

He's thirty, forty, fifty. He's in a mine, in a lab, at sea, in a basement, on the ice. I stand behind him, somewhere at the very edge of hearing, and when he reaches for something he won't come back from, I tell him the same thing every time:

"Don't."

Sometimes he listens.
