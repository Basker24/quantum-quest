I grab him under the arms and pull. He doesn't scream, just breathes out through his teeth. Something below holds. I brace my foot against the bulkhead and pull again, harder, and something in my back tears, hot, and below it holds, the way an anchor holds.

Behind me, from the cabin, it suddenly gets bright. I look back. Under the cabin ceiling there's fire, yellow, along the wiring, along the canvas of the jump seats, and it's coming toward us, into the cockpit door, because the window above us draws like a stovepipe. The smoke turns black and thick. I'm breathing it. Vitka coughs in my arms and says, "Andryukh, get out. Get out, you idiot."

I don't get out. I pull, and below it holds. Then I'm lying on top of him, with fire above us, and it's so hot the hair on my head crackles. The last thing I see is his boot under the pedal, tarpaulin, wide in the shaft, and the foot sitting loose inside it. It smells of burnt rubber and of coal dust from Mom's hair, like when I was little and she leaned over me before sleep.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm on my knees in the cockpit again, and Vitka is hanging in his harness saying, "Give me a yank, huh? Only a hard one."

My hair is crackling. I touch my head, and it's all there. I remember how the fire came from the cabin toward us, into the chimney of the window, and how I lay on top of Vitka. I remember his boot.

I was pulling him by the shoulders, and the boot stayed put, tarpaulin, with a shaft up to the knee. His foot moved freely inside it; I could feel that through his trouser leg when I yanked.
