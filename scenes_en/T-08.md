In April there's a methane explosion at the Glubokaya mine. I find out in algebra. A guy in the back row's Nokia beeps, he reads the text, then looks at me, and his face goes so strange that I get up and walk out of class without asking anybody. Twelve men are still underground, and Dad is one of them.

For three days and nights all of Rudnichny stands outside the pithead building. Women in puffy coats, a handwritten list pinned to the board, the prosecutor, a TV crew from Novosibirsk. One of them, the one with the microphone, goes after Mom: "What are you feeling right now?" I say a word to him that on any other day would get me slapped on the mouth by Mom. Today she doesn't hear. She stands there holding Sonka by the hood so she won't run off. The first evening I stand next to her and count the names on the list every hour, and there are still twelve. Where I was for the other two days, ask me even now, and I won't be able to answer right away.

::if F_Т01 == БУБЛИК
Bublik howls in the yard three nights running, and none of the neighbors yell at him.
::end

On the first day they bring up six, then nobody for a long time. On the fourth morning I'm at the pithead again when the cage brings up three more. The first one isn't Dad; you can tell by the walk before you can see the face. Dad comes out second. He's gray as plaster, and he bums a cigarette off somebody, though his whole life he's only ever smoked his own. Mom doesn't run to him. She squats down right in the mud and covers her face with her hands. And my knee starts jumping by itself, and I can't make it stop.

That night I get up for a drink of water. Dad is sitting in the kitchen in the dark, in his undershirt, a cigarette in his fingers, unlit. The clock over the stove says four-twelve.

::if F_Т01 == ЧАСЫ
Exactly what my watch says, the one from the backwater. For some reason I put my hand behind my back.
::end

"Down there I could feel I was dead, Andryukha," he says quietly, without turning around. "Three times I felt it. And then I'm standing by the crosscut, alive, breathing."

::if F_Т01 == БУБЛИК
Bublik growls at him from under the table, the first and last time in his life.
::end

Then he gets up and goes out on the balcony to smoke. He never talks about it again, not then, not later.

> Of the twelve, nine came up alive. Every Pokrov after that Dad remembered the other three, along with the two from ninety-one.

Later on, people often asked me where I was those three days. I always kept it short.

## После A
The leader of the mine rescue squad, gray-haired Palych (everybody calls him that, from his patronymic), has a voice like it's coming out of a barrel. First he chases me off, and then he lets me haul tanks and water. For three days I sleep in snatches on a bench in their locker room.

On the third night Palych teaches me which way to run if there's smoke in the tunnel. "Into the draft, got it? Always into the draft." I keep repeating it to myself until I fall asleep.

## После B
I make soup out of whatever's in the fridge, put Sonka to bed, and lie to her that Dad's gone fishing. Mom sits in the kitchen for three nights in her coat and never takes it off.

I don't cry once in front of them. Later I was very proud of that, though there was nothing to be proud of.

## После C
Every night I go to the backwater and sit on the bank. I don't go in, I just talk to the water out loud, like an idiot: "Take me instead."

The water doesn't answer. On the fourth morning I run from the river to the pithead, and the cage has already brought up three.

## После D
In the smoking room by the pithead I hear a foreman say under his breath that the methane sensors in the section were taped over so they could make their quota. I find the reporter from Novosibirsk and tell her everything.

Afterward I'm in bed for three days with a fever, because I stood in the wind for three days and nights. The story goes out on TV, and the mine director remembers the name Samokhin.
