The path over the headland runs across the rocks, up and down, past concrete steps that end for no reason at all. The ladies from Belovo hold on to the back of my T-shirt, like children in kindergarten. My flashlight is the one on my Nokia; it lights half a meter. Below on the right the sea roars; you can't see it, only hear it.

At a bend the rock under my flip-flop is wet and smooth as soap. I don't even have time to throw out my arms. The T-shirt rips in one lady's fist.

I don't fall long. Down, backward, into the dark, and I hit the rocks twice, first my shoulder, then the back of my head. I hardly feel the second blow, only hear it, dry, like a watermelon dropped on asphalt.

I'm lying in a rock pool, in warm water up to my ears. I can't move anything. Above me the ladies call, "Andrei! Andryusha!", and then they call more quietly, then they stop. From the beach past the headland the music pounds, steady, like a pump. The water in the pool is rising; it's the tide; it's reached my chin, then my lips. The last thing, though there's nowhere for it to come from, is the smell of Mom's hair.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing on the sand again, and the bucket is at my feet, and the ladies from Belovo want to go back to the bungalow.

The back of my head is humming. I feel it; there's nothing there, no bump, no blood. My fingers are wet, but it's sweat.

I remember the wet rock at the bend and the sound my head made meeting the rock below. I remember how they called me from above, quieter and quieter. They left; they decided their guide had abandoned them. Maybe that's for the best. I'm never leading anyone over those rocks again.
