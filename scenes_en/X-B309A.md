The numbers race, and I look at them and don't understand what they mean. Pressure up, up, then down for a second. Good, I think, it's easing. I tell Aisen, "Keep recording. One more minute." Aisen looks at me with white eyes, then drops the laptop and runs. Sardaana shouts something at me, pulls at my sleeve; I pull my sleeve free.

The mound under me rises, all at once, like a whale's back. I see the turf on it split, the crack fly open, and a column of white steam shoot out of it. There's no sound. I'm lifted and thrown, and I fly along with chunks of ice, with peat, with tripods, and land on my back in the moss, far away.

I lie there, and the cloud settles over me. It's warm and stinks, and you can't breathe it, but I do, because there's nothing else. My chest burns. I want to get up and can't remember how it's done. The sky above me is white, then gray, then it's gone. It smells of rotten eggs, of swamp and, very close, of coal dust from Mom's hair.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing by the mound again, and Aisen is saying, "Methane forty percent. Forty-five."

My chest burns from inside; I cough, and Sardaana looks at me. I remember how the pressure dropped for a second and I decided it was easing. It wasn't easing. It was taking a breath.

This mound doesn't have a minute left, not one. We have to leave, now, and take everyone, and let the instruments stay.
