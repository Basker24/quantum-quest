In December Vitka steals Uncle Tolya's Lada Nine. Not for keeps, just for the night. Uncle Tolya is on a bender, the keys are hanging on a nail in the front hall, and across the river there's a disco at the club, and the girls from School No. 110 will be there. You can't take the bridge, the traffic cops are there. So it's across the ice, straight over.

"The ice is solid," says Vitka. "Some guys drove over in a Niva yesterday. I saw it myself."

It's thirty-one below outside, and yesterday it was nine below. The Nine starts on the third try and breathes steam like a cow. Ruki Vverh! is blasting out of the tape deck, the cheesy dance pop everybody says they hate. Vitka sits behind the wheel in Uncle Tolya's fur hat with the earflaps, trying to find first gear.

I'm standing by the passenger door. The door is open, and Vitka's waiting.

::if ДАР >= 10
The back of my neck goes cold, though there's no wind. For a second, like after a camera flash, I see the green glow of the dashboard lights, only for some reason they're underwater, and Vitka's red hair is floating up.
::end

"You coming or what?" Vitka yells. "The slow dances are about to start and we're out here freezing our snot off."

## После A
I yank the keys out of the ignition and stuff them in my pocket. Vitka yells and comes at me, then kicks a tire and walks off without his hat, in thirty-one below.

For a week he doesn't even say hi to me. On the eighth day he comes over to make up, with a bottle of Baltika beer, one for the two of us.

## После B
About twenty meters from the far bank the Nine goes through the ice nose first. Water pours in over our feet, Vitka is swearing, and suddenly I say to him, very calmly, "Out the window. Now." I don't know how I know.

We climb out through the windows, crawl across the ice on our bellies, and run home in our wet clothes, which are frozen stiff as boards by the time we get to our building. They pull the Nine out with a tractor three days later. Uncle Tolya never finds out Vitka was the one driving.

## После C
I run to the pay phone by the store and call Dad. He hitches a ride, gets there in fifteen minutes, and pulls Vitka out of the car by the collar.

Afterward Uncle Tolya whips Vitka with his belt, and the police put Vitka on their list of juvenile delinquents. Vitka only says one thing to me: "You're a real rat, Andryukha."

## После D
I tell Vitka what I just saw: the green dashboard lights underwater, and his hair. I say it quietly, and I guess the way I say it makes Vitka go pale and switch off the engine.

We sit in the cooling car for about ten minutes without talking. Then he gets out and says, "Screw you, Vodyanoy," and we walk home. He hangs the keys back on their nail.
