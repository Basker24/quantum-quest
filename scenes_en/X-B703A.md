The footpath along the building is polished like a mirror; the stroller rolls along it by itself, you only have to hold on. I walk right under the wall, where it's less slippery, and look at Varya's nose in her snowsuit bag.

There's a short crack overhead. The blow lands on my neck and shoulder at once, so I don't even hear it; I'm just lying with my cheek on the ice. My arms are somewhere apart from me, I can't feel them. Next to me pieces of ice glitter, clear, with a birch leaf frozen inside one.

I've let go of the stroller. It's rolling downhill, toward the road, slowly, bouncing over the ruts, and I watch it with one eye, because the other one is in the snow. I can't shout; my mouth is warm and salty. The stroller rolls, and I lie there watching until I can't see anymore.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing at the corner of the building again, holding the stroller handle, and Varya smacks her lips in her sleep.

I remember lying with my cheek on the ice and not feeling my arms. Now I'm squeezing the stroller handle so hard the plastic creaks, and my arms feel it, both of them.

I remember the stroller rolling toward the road, bouncing, and I couldn't shout. At the edge of the roof over the footpath hangs an icicle as tall as I am. A minute ago I didn't even look at it.

My neck aches on the right side, in one spot, and I rub it with my mitten. Along the wall old ice splinters are frozen into the packed snow, a whole strip of them along the entire building. In the middle of the yard the snow is knee-deep and untouched, and there isn't a single splinter in it.
