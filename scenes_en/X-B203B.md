I get to the park fast, because I'm angry and strong, and at first the frost seems funny to me. By the third kilometer the soles of my Adidas are like the plastic of a ruler. They don't bend, and with every step I feel the ice under my heel all in one piece.

The path runs along the very edge, above the Tom. Down below, about twenty meters, is the white riverbed, with steam rising off it like a pot. At the turn by the bench my right foot slides forward without a sound. I grab a bush, the bush snaps like glass, and I slide down on my back, faster and faster, until the slope runs out.

At the bottom I lie on the ice by the bank and can't get up: my right leg is folded under me the wrong way. First it hurts, then it's cold, then it's so warm I want to take off my jacket. The steam from the river spreads white over me, and I feel good in it.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing outside Lyokha's entrance again, and Lena is picking at the frost on the railing with her mitten.

My toes are burning inside my Adidas, as if I'd already frozen them to the bone. I look down and see white sneakers with three stripes, bought with freight-car money.

My mistake wasn't today. My mistake was that first fall, at the Tomsk-2 yard, when I threw out my old boots because in new sneakers my shoulders look wider. In Lyokha's front hall stand his dad's felt boots with galoshes.
