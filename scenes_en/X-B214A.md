I lie down without undressing, on top of the bedspread. The pillow smells of somebody else's detergent. My head doesn't ache in one spot anymore, it aches all over, and the nausea comes in waves, in time with the water heater behind the wall.

An hour later I wake up because I've thrown up on the pillow. I need to get up and open the window; I know that. Only my hand lies on the blanket and won't lift, as if it's gone to sleep, and I look at it for a long time. The blue window of the water heater glows in the dark kitchen, its reflections trembling on the ceiling. I start counting them and lose count at seven.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm sitting on the edge of the couch in my bathrobe again, my head aching, the water heater humming behind the wall.

I remember the hand on the blanket, heavy and not mine, and losing count at seven. I remember the sour smell of the pillow, though the pillow now is clean and smells of detergent.

I'm unsteady, but my legs do what I tell them. I'm not lying down on this couch tonight.
