The ceiling settles on my back, and I crawl. The tanks ahead of me, in my hands, my own on my back. The silt is so thick I close my eyes; it makes no difference. In the middle of the gap something jerks me back.

The valve of the tank on my back has caught on a rock. I lunge forward, and it holds. I push back, and the ceiling presses down on my shoulder blades and won't let me back either. I feel behind my back with one hand, find the rock, the valve, and my fingers slide over them in the silt. I let go of the rope to feel around. Where it is now, I don't know.

I'm breathing fast. Too fast, I know that, and I can't stop. The pressure gauge glows in front of my mask; the needle creeps toward the red, a hundred bar, fifty, twenty. Rock under my belly, rock over my back, rock to the left. My shoulders are pinned so tight I couldn't take a deep breath even if there were anything to breathe.

The regulator sucks on nothing, with a whistle. There's no more air. I hold on for some time longer, counting to myself, and as I count someone starts counting with me, many voices, close, like under the snag when I was seven. It smells of Mom's hair. The rock under my cheek is warm, like the sand by the backwater.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing at the mouth of the squeeze again, knee-deep in rust-colored slurry, and the marine is pointing at me.

My shoulders are cramped. I raise my arms over my head, and they go up; nothing is holding them. Behind, between my shoulder blades, it aches as if from a blow.

I remember how the valve on my back stuck against the rock and held. I remember the whistle of the empty regulator and the warm rock under my cheek. I reach behind my back, and my fingers hit the valve, higher than my shoulders.
