"Synchronize watches, Vadim Petrovich," I say. "Four-twelve."

He flinches as if struck. He heard those words live only once in his life, over the mine phone from the minus six hundred forty level, and after that only on tape.

::if F_Т01 == ЧАСЫ || F_ЧАСЫ_ЛУНЁВА
"Yours stopped on the twenty-eighth of January '86," I say. "On the back, 'Synchronize watches. 12.04.61,' and a scratch on the glass."
::end

::if F_ЧАСЫ_ЛУНЁВА
"You keep it on you, but you can't bring yourself to put it on your wrist."
::end

He slowly takes the watch out of his pocket, Granddad's, and looks at it as if seeing it for the first time. Then he sits down right on the sand.

I tell him everything; he doesn't interrupt. That Mitya is lying here, in a flooded working under Krasnaya Gorka, where the river drains through the old adit, and other worlds have nothing to do with it. He needs to be buried. I saw him on the bank last night, among my own, looking down at his feet. That in two years, during the default, people from the Orpheus foundation will come to him, and he mustn't sell them anything. That the boy whose blood he'll take at the hospital tomorrow owes nothing to him or to the world. And that the boy will grow up, if no one gets in his way.

"Who are you?" asks Lunyov.

"Samokhin. The one who pulled him out. And the one who was pulled out."

Over the Tom it's still dark; only at the edge of the sky, over Krasnaya Gorka, is it graying a little. I sit on the sand leaning against the snag, listening to the water lap. My heart stops at four-twelve, quietly, with no fork and no Choir.

I have nowhere to rewind to, and no reason to.
