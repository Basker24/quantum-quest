The Choir in me goes out, slowly, one voice at a time, the way the windows in the building across the street go dark when everyone goes to bed. By autumn my head is quiet. The tuning fork was struck in '96; now it's for that boy on the sand to ring, and in me it has finished sounding.

I live eleven more years, the most ordinary ones. My knees ache and medicines get more expensive. The Tom returns to its bed, and boats run on it again. I no longer wake at 04:12; I sleep till seven, like any normal pensioner.
