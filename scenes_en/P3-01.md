I don't leave the seam. I step onto it, the way kids step onto a rail, arms spread, and hold my balance. The four minutes while the two ends of my life touch stretch out. The noon beyond the bushes freezes: the boy is coughing and never finishes the cough; the girl has opened her mouth and never says "two hundred and fifty." And I stand and don't grow old.

The Choir comes out of the bushes.

They're not voices anymore. They stand in the willows, and I see their faces, all mine. One has burned hands, another a miner's lamp on his helmet, a third a diving mask around his neck. Someone very young, in a Pioneer neckerchief, someone older than me. The ones who died before my eyes in this life I recognize at once: they stand closer than the rest and look more intently.

"Did you sell us all, or save us?" asks one, young, with coal dust in the wrinkles around his eyes. "We never figured it out."
::end

::if F_ПУТЬ == ПАРТИЯ || F_ПУТЬ == ТЁПЛАЯ_ВОДА || F_ПУТЬ == СЕМЬЯ || F_ПУТЬ == ХОР
"Did you listen to us, or put up with us?" asks one, young, tanned, with a peeling nose. "We never figured it out."

::if F_ЗНАЕТ_ДЕДА
From the side, from a crack I'd taken for a branch, sounds a low voice with a choirmaster's drawl: "Synchronize watches." Granddad isn't in the Choir. He stands on the other side, in his own crack, waiting to see what I'll do. "Step aside, Andryusha," he says. "You've had enough."
::end

How long I can hold on here, I don't know. Maybe a minute. Maybe a hundred thousand minutes. The Choir waits.
