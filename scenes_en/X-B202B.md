I pry the doors apart with my hands; they give with a creak. Above is a half-meter gap, and beyond it the third-floor landing and somebody's shoes. I pull myself up, get through to the waist, and then the elevator, which it turns out nobody cut the power to, remembers it needs to go up.

It all happens fast. It only hurts for one second.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing in the elevator between the second and third floors again. The bulb is flickering. The flask's lid is hissing.

I grab my stomach, because I know for sure it isn't there. My stomach is there, my sweater whole. The shoes beyond the gap were brown, by the way; I remember that from somewhere.

The mist creeps across the floor.

The elevator moved while I was hanging in the gap, by itself; somebody called it from upstairs. I remember the panel inside, a row of buttons with worn-off numbers and one red one, set apart, like in the elevators we rode as kids, pressing every button except that one.
