"Mikhalych, take a look."

He comes over and shines his lamp on the crack. We stand side by side, two helmets, two spots of light on the coal. Mikhalych says, "Uh—" and doesn't get to say anything else.

Gas comes out of the crack, ahead of the coal. You can't see it or hear it; my ears just suddenly pop, like in the cage. The air goes thick and empty, like in a jar with everything pumped out of it. I open my mouth, and there's nothing in it to breathe. I grab for the self-rescuer on my belt, and my fingers can't find the clasp. Mikhalych sits down on the floor, slowly, as if he's tired.

The coal comes later. I can't see it anymore, I only hear it rustling over my head, steady, like rain on a garage roof. Under my cheek there's cold grit, and suddenly it's crowded and calm, like that time under the snag, where it was crowded with voices. The voices are here, close by, and they seem glad to see me.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing at the face again with the jackhammer in my hands. Mikhalych is listening to the seam with his head cocked.

I'm breathing through my mouth, loudly, like after a run, and I can't get enough. There's air. There's even plenty of it. I slap my belt, find the clasp of the self-rescuer and keep a finger on it.

I'm not going to call Mikhalych over. He's standing three steps away, alive, with his shovel, and he doesn't know that a moment ago he was sitting on the floor next to me.

I remember it got hard to breathe before the seam moved. The coal was still standing like a wall, and the props were already swimming in front of my eyes, and Mikhalych sat down on the floor without a word, the way you sit down for a smoke.
