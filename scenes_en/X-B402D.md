I run to the truck along the dock and climb into the back. Here the doors are wide open, no spring; they won't close by themselves. I sit down behind the boxes at the very back and wait for the flashlight to go.

The flashlight goes. Then the driver comes. I hear him arguing with the watchman about the Gazelle, about thieves, about how "you'd let them carry off the whole place." Then he slams both doors, left and right, and drops the bar on the outside. The bar clangs. I yell and pound the wall, but the engine is already roaring, and the truck pulls away.

It's twenty below in the back, and it's moving. I'm thrown across the boxes at every pothole. An hour later I get warm, like that other time, and I know what it means, and I can't do anything. I lie on the boxes, cheek on cardboard, and the chick in the hat smiles at me in the dark; I can't see him, but I know he's there. It smells of coal dust and Mom.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing in the doorway of the cold room again with the forty-second box in my arms, and the flashlight is crawling across the yard.

I remember the bar. The clang outside, left door, right door. I remember being thrown across the boxes on the Novosibirsk highway when I could no longer lift my hand.

I remembered it right: the cold-room door slams shut. But what I made of it was stupid. On a truck the driver shuts the doors, and the truck drives off with you inside. A fridge is a fridge, wheels or no wheels.
