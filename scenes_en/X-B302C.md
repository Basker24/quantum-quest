I lie down in the hollow, face down, beside the stream, and press myself against the rocks. Here I'm lower than everything around. Let it hit the cedar, the boulder, wherever it likes. The stream runs right by my face, thin, cold; I can hear it gurgling.

Then it stops gurgling and starts to roar. The water rises in a minute, first to my elbows, then to my shoulders, brown, icy, and something knocks in it, louder and louder, as if someone were rolling empty barrels down the chute from above. I get up on my knees.

The first block hits me in the side, and I fall face first into the water. I'm dragged downhill with the gravel, flipped over, slammed against rocks with my shoulder, my back, my head. Water in my mouth, in my nose, icy, with sand. I don't know which way is up. The scar on my palm snags on something and tears off with the skin. The last thing I feel is a smell. Even here, in icy water, it smells of coal dust and Mom.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing on the saddle in the downpour again, and the hammer above my shoulder is humming like a transformer.

There's sand in my mouth. I spit, and there's none; my mouth is clean, only my tongue remembers. I remember how the stream stopped gurgling and started to roar. The rocks in the hollow are smooth, water-worn, and higher up on the sides lie sharp ones, covered in lichen.

And water again. I ran from it into the mountains, and it found me here, twelve years later, in a chute two steps wide.
