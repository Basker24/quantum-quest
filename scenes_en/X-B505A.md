I grab her by the shoulders, from the front, and she kicks me in the face with her fin. My mask flies off. The world turns blurry and salty; my eyes sting. Her hand finds my regulator and yanks it out of my mouth; she needs air, any air, even mine.

I breathe in water. Seawater, cold at thirty meters. I cough it out, and the cough is water too. My body tears upward on its own, toward the window in the ceiling, and I don't hold it back; I don't have a single thought for that. My chest is clenched, and I don't breathe out. I hold in the last thing I managed to breathe, and fly up with it.

At the surface my chest explodes from inside, under the ribs on the right, as if someone had shoved a red-hot poker in there. I grab the boat's ladder. Foam comes out of my mouth, pink, and drips on the step. Ploy shouts something from above and pulls me by the collar.

My hands let go of the ladder by themselves. The last thing I see is the sky, very blue, and a gull. The last thing I hear is whispering, as if from under water, many voices, close, and it smells of Mom's hair, though here it smells only of diesel.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm hanging at thirty meters again, by the gray rock, and Vika is taking the regulator out of her mouth.

My chest hurts under the ribs. I take a breath from my regulator, slowly, and the air comes in, dry, tasting of rubber. The bubbles go up evenly.

I remember her heel in my mask, from the front. I remember the foam on the boat's step and how my fingers let go of the ladder on their own. The bridge of my nose aches, as if from a heel.
