I put on the turn signal and pull into the oncoming lane. The semi beside me is as long as a fence, and the snow dust off it hits the windshield so hard the wipers just smear the white around. I floor it. The Granta roars and picks up speed slowly, too slowly.

Out of the white ahead come two headlights, high up, at the height of my head. I manage to jerk the wheel right, toward the semi, and the KAMAZ doesn't hit us head-on, it hits my door.

Glass pours into my lap like grain. The steering wheel is pressed into my chest, and I can't take another breath, however hard I try. In the back Varya is screaming, and Kolka is crying in his bass voice, the way he always does when he's woken up. I listen to them and try to take one more breath.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm driving behind the semi on the straight before Yurga again, and the wipers are chasing snow dust across the glass.

I remember two headlights at the height of my head and the wheel in my chest. I slide the seat back one notch, and it gets easier to breathe, though the wheel has nothing to do with it.

I remember Varya screaming and Kolka crying, and I couldn't breathe in to tell them everything was all right. Varya asks, "Dad, are we gonna pass it?" There's been no oncoming traffic for a long time.
