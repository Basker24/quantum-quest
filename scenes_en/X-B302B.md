I run down the boulder field, from block to block, the way I used to run across the garage roofs in Rudnichny. The rocks are wet; the rusty lichen on them is slippery as soap. Three hundred meters to the forest. The hammer above my shoulder hums louder and thinner, and it seems to me that it's singing. I run, and it sings in my ear.

Then the song breaks off. White comes from every side at once, and I'm lifted and thrown, the way you throw a sack into a truck bed. I don't remember the blow, only flying. The blocks pass beneath me sideways.

I'm lying between two rocks, head downhill, and water is running over me. My legs won't obey. My head is lying on the rock somehow wrong, too far to one side, and I can't lift it. The hammer lies nearby, on the next block, and its pick is still glowing a little, blue. Then it goes out. It smells of wet rock and coal dust, Mom's hair.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing on the saddle in the downpour again, and the hammer above my shoulder is humming like a transformer.

I remember how it sang. Thinner, thinner, and then it broke off, and the strike came at once. I was running down an open slope, and the only thing higher than me on the whole boulder field was that pick above my shoulder.

The shoulder the hammer hangs on aches like a tooth in cold water. The hammer is on it now too, and the pick above my shoulder sticks up higher than my head.
