I never did answer Sonka. Not in '55, not later. Her voice messages came once a week at first, then once a month, then stopped. Flights to Russia resumed in '60, and I bought a ticket and returned it two days before departure, and why, I couldn't explain even to myself. For me Mom stayed in our kitchen in the five-story block in Rudnichny, forty years old, with coal dust in her hair. It seemed to me it would be better for her that way. It was better for me.

At three o'clock I start the boat and head out from Rawai into open sea, a couple of kilometers. I cut the motor. The water lies smooth as in a basin, not a wave, not a breath of wind. The comet hangs over the northern horizon, low, bright, with a tail that, it seems to me, points north. "It'll come back on the twenty-eighth of July 2061," Galina Ivanovna said at graduation, and we cracked up: seventy-two was never.

I'm drawn where the tail points. It has drawn me all this last year, the way you're drawn home after long work. I sit on the bench holding the gunwale with both hands. The storm jacket lies beside me, folded; I took it out of the wardrobe for some reason.

It's four in the morning in Kemerovo too. We're in the same time zone; I learned that in 2020, at Dad's funeral, and I've always remembered it since. At two minutes to four-twelve the whispering under the water rises to the very surface, and I hear it without water, loudly, all the voices at once. They don't call. They wait, the way you wait on a platform for a train that's already been announced.

At four-twelve they all go silent at once.

The sea under the boat grows light, though there's nothing to make it light. For half a minute a sunny day stands in the water; I see sand on the bottom and a white snag that can't possibly be here, in the Andaman Sea. Then it all goes dark. The whispering is gone. For the first time since I was seven my head is completely quiet.

I take out my phone and write to Sonka, for the first time in six years: "Sonya, tell Mom I'm on my way back from the river." I press "send." A little circle spins above the letters.
