I run straight across the ice, and about ten meters out the ice goes out from under my feet. The water is so cold that at first it feels hot. My jacket drags me down; I grab the edge, and the edge crumbles like a cookie. The current takes me by the legs, calmly, like it owns me, and I go under the ice almost without a sound. The last thing I see is gray light above me, cloudy, as if through frosted glass, and a dark spot at the edge: Timur's backpack.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing on the embankment again, and the ice under Timur is sagging with a quiet, wet sound. The backpack at the edge of the hole, twenty meters away.

I'm forty years old, and I've drowned in the Tom again, thirty-three years after the first time. The water is the same and pulls the same way. I'm not running straight across ever again, I don't care what happens.
