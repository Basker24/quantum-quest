The Tom has been dry for the third summer running. Where in ninety-six I was scared to swim out past the buoys, people now walk. Stones, a rusty old Lada lying wheels-up, dust as white as the flour in the humanitarian-aid sacks. From Kemerovo all the way to Tomsk the riverbed is dry, and people walk their dogs down the shipping channel.

The backwater is still where it always was: by the left bank, under the willow thickets, behind the spit with the old pilings. Round, forty meters across, full to the brim and black. Scientists from Tomsk spent three summers running sonar over it, and their report says it has no bottom. They could have saved the grant money and asked me. It has a bottom. I've lain on it.

A comet hangs over the riverbed. Its tail lies exactly along the river; you could set a compass by it. In eleventh grade our astronomy teacher promised us this comet. "You'll be seventy-two. Live that long, and you'll see it." I lived that long, Galina Ivanovna. Here I am, sitting on a rock, looking.

How many times I've died, I honestly don't remember. You'd have to ask them; they remember everything. Before dawn you can hardly hear them, just a hiss, like Grandma's old Rekord TV after the last station went off the air.

The snag sticks up in the same place, white and smooth, its branches spread like the fingers of a hand. Sixty-five years ago I couldn't hold on to it.

It's eighteen minutes to four, and there's time before dawn to tell it all in order, starting with that July when I was seven and had never died yet.
