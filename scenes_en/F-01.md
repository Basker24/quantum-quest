I don't go back to 2061. I take a step sideways out of the willows, to where the seam has almost closed. It's a narrow crack between worlds so improbable that no one but me lives in them. There I don't grow old, and death there has no fork I don't know.

The Choir enters me all at once and goes silent, because there's no one left for it to call. Everyone who died instead of me is in me now, and I carry them the way you carry a heavy fur coat you can't bear to throw away.

::if F_ЗНАЕТ_ДЕДА
One voice doesn't enter me. It sounds from the side, from the next crack, low, with a choirmaster's drawl: "Synchronize watches." I answer: "Four-twelve." We'll never meet; in these worlds everyone lives alone. But now I know where Granddad went in January '86.
::end

Behind me the world collapses along the most probable path, the one with the "second chance," the Wager and the dry Tom. I'm no longer in it. Those who remembered me stop remembering, gently and without pain, the way you forget a dream by lunchtime.

::end

Vitka gets up from the stone, leaning on his cane, and looks at the water for a long time. He doesn't remember whom he was waiting for. He walks home slowly, across the whole riverbed, and twice on the way he stops and looks back, as if someone had called him.

I live a long time. In my time they tear down School No. 41 in Kemerovo and put up something glass in its place. In my time the backwater grows over, dries up and fills again. I meet the comet on the same stone by the same water for the fifth time. In all that time the scar on my palm never fades. There's long been no one left to call me by my name.

I cast my line into the black water. The float twitches.

"A bite," I say, and out of habit look around for someone to tell.

! Ending "The One Who Stayed." Immortality in solitude. My Life shows what other roads led to this morning.
