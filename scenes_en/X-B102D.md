I drop the jackhammer and run to the drive while the seam is still only crackling. Seryoga is standing by the frame, finishing his verse. I knock him to the floor, under the frame, and lie down beside him.

"What the hell are you doing?!"

Behind us something sighs, and the coal comes along the face in a rustling wave. The iron frame of the drive stands over Seryoga, and the coal flows around it on both sides, the way water flows around a bridge pier. A hollow stays under the frame, and that's where he breathes. There's room for one, and I'm lying half outside, up to my waist.

The coal settles on my legs, then on my back, layer after layer, cold and heavy. Seryoga holds my hand under the frame and shouts. I hear him until the coal reaches the back of my head.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing at the face again with the jackhammer in my hands. Seryoga is singing off-key by the drive.

They really did dig him out alive, under the frame; I remembered that right. I remember his hand holding mine, warm, in a fingerless glove, squeezing every time a new layer settled on me. One man fits under that frame, and that man is Seryoga.

The fact that Seryoga survived didn't tell me anything; I made that up myself. I just wanted to lie down where somebody didn't die. I bang the jackhammer against a prop in anger, and Mikhalych turns around at the sound.
