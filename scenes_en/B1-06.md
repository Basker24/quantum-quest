The wedding is at the Beryozka in Rudnichny, in a hall with a mirror ball and an MC from Leninsk who in every pause yells "Gorko!" as if he's being murdered. Seryoga is in a black suit he bought to grow into about five years ago, and he keeps fixing his tie. Nika is in white. Vitka is best man, with a WITNESS sash across his chest, and he's already lost the rings twice, both times in his own pocket.

They met three years ago at Vitka's garages. I was sitting right there and watched it happen. Nika was counting skewers then, and then she stopped counting.

::if F_В101 == НИКА
For two years I called her every evening. Then every other evening. Then on holidays. In January she called me herself and said, "Samokhin, Seryoga proposed. Are you happy for me?" I said I was, and then sat for half an hour holding the phone with the dial tone humming in it.
::end

::if F_В105 == ВГСЧ
Seryoga is still at the face, a team leader on Dad's crew. When I come to the Glubokaya with the unit for an inspection, he yells across the whole drift, "Rescuer! Rescue me from the foreman!"
::end

::if F_В105 == БРИГАДА
Seryoga is on our team, team leader; Dad picked him himself. We've been shoulder to shoulder underground for five years now, and I know how he breathes when he's tired and how he sings when he's scared.
::end

After ten Nika comes out on the steps, takes off her shoes and sits down on a step. I'm standing there too, smoking, though I quit. Vitka is by the door on the phone with the MC, who left early, and he's swearing.

"What's wrong with you, Samokhin?" asks Nika. "You sat there all evening like it was a wake."

"Tired," I say. "Had a duty shift."

She looks up at me for a long time, the way she looked in '96 when only the ears were left by the snag. Then she says:

"Twenty-six steps in your stairwell. I still remember. Seryoga doesn't know, but I remember."

Vitka hangs up and turns to us.

## После A
"To you two," I say, and raise a glass I'm not holding. Nika laughs, and Vitka shoves his into my hand.

In the hall I dance with her aunt from Novosibirsk, the one with the dacha. The aunt leads and tells me in my ear that when Nika was little she talked everyone's ears off about me, "Andryusha this, Andryusha that." I step on her foot. She says never mind, both her feet are used to it.

At three in the morning Seryoga, drunk and happy, hugs me by the taxi and says, "You'll be godfather to our first, got it? You pulled me out of that face." I promise and put him in the taxi head first, so he won't bump it.

## После B
"I love you," I say. "Since the backwater. Twenty-six steps, Nika. I remember too."

It goes so quiet you can hear somebody in the hall putting on Laskovy Mai, the old boy-band hit. Nika doesn't get up off the step. She puts on her shoes, one, then the other, slowly, and only then says:

"You kept quiet for eighteen years, Samokhin. You could have managed one more evening."

She goes back into the hall, to Seryoga. Vitka comes right up to me, his sash twisted. He doesn't hit me. He says quietly, "That's my sister. And that's my friend. And who are you?" Then he leaves too. For the rest of the evening he doesn't speak to me, and for seven years after that he hardly does.

## После C
I drink vodka straight from the bottle behind the garages past the Beryozka and catch a taxi to the Glubokaya. The driver takes me to the gates and won't take my money: "Go on, miner."

::if F_В105 == ВГСЧ
I don't have a night shift, I'm a rescuer; my next duty is the day after tomorrow. But my legs carry me to the lamp room, the way they always have.
::end

The night shift is on in the lamp room, and Tanya is behind the window. I hand her my tag. She looks at me, at the tag, and puts it away in a drawer.

"I'm not giving a lamp to a drunk," she says. "Kill me if you like."

Then she opens the side door and lets me in, behind the racks, where it smells of electrolyte and hot plastic. She pours me tea from her thermos. I sit on a crate and tell her everything: the backwater, the steps, November, the way coal flows. She listens and changes lamps in their slots. Toward morning I fall asleep right there on the crate, my head on her quilted jacket, and Tanya hands out lamps until the end of the shift, trying not to make any noise.
