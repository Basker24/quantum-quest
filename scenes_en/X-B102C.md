I drop the jackhammer and run. The face is low, I have to run bent over, my helmet knocks against the props, knock, knock, like a stick dragged along a fence. Behind me something sighs, and then starts rustling, louder and louder, and the rustling catches up with me.

It hits me behind the knees and knocks my legs out, like a wave on the Tom when the Zarya hydrofoil goes by. I'm thrown onto the conveyor, face down on the belt, and the belt is moving, and coal piles onto my back, layer after layer. I still try to crawl. My hands scrape at the rubber; my legs aren't mine anymore, something heavy and cold is holding them.

Seryoga's lamp shines right in my eyes; he's shouting something, and I can't make out the words. Then the coal reaches the back of my head. It gets dark and quiet, and it smells of Mom's hair.

## Пробуждение
…and I'm standing at the face again with the jackhammer in my hands. Seryoga is singing off-key by the drive.

My back hurts as if the seam were still lying on it. I turn around and look back along the face, the way I ran. Twenty meters to the drive. I didn't make it. I made twelve; I remember by the props.

Mikhalych is listening to the seam and doesn't know anything. But in my ears it's still rustling.

I started running when the seam breathed out. Before that it was shooting for a whole minute, click, click, and I spent that minute standing next to Mikhalych, listening. By the way, they dug Seryoga out alive by the drive; I remember that too, from somewhere.
